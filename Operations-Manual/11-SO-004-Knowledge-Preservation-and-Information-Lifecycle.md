# Standing Order SO-004

## Knowledge Preservation and Information Lifecycle Policy

**Status:** Active  
**Authority:** Commander Spock, Chief Science Officer  
**Approved by:** Captain John  
**Effective:** 2026-08-11

## Purpose

This policy ensures that knowledge created by USS Zupp1 accumulates rather than disappears.

> Every important lesson should only need to be learned once.

## Philosophy

Slack exists for conversation. The Book exists for knowledge.

Conversation is temporary. Knowledge is permanent. No crew member should need to rediscover information that another officer has already established.

## Responsibilities

### Commander Spock

Commander Spock serves as Chief Knowledge Officer and is responsible for:

- maintaining The Book;
- identifying permanent knowledge;
- preserving institutional memory;
- updating standards;
- removing or reclassifying obsolete standards;
- maintaining cross-references.

Commander Spock determines what deserves permanent preservation. Routine archival decisions require no further approval.

### Commander One

The Executive Officer is responsible for:

- preserving engineering knowledge;
- documenting reusable engineering patterns;
- maintaining technical standards;
- identifying engineering documentation suitable for The Book.

### Lieutenant Commander Data (Ro)

The Intelligence Officer is responsible for:

- preserving enduring research conclusions;
- identifying validated intelligence;
- documenting assumptions that became evidence;
- recording unresolved intelligence gaps.

### Chief Engineer Scotty

The Chief Engineer is responsible for:

- documenting implementation decisions;
- notifying Commander Spock when engineering work creates reusable knowledge;
- identifying technical procedures that should become Engineering Standards.

### Captain

The Captain has no routine archival duties. The Captain's responsibility is leadership; knowledge preservation is delegated.

## Continuous Knowledge Review

Commander Spock shall periodically review:

- Slack;
- GitHub;
- engineering documentation;
- research reports;
- mission summaries;
- Captain's decisions.

The review is continuous rather than event-driven. The objective is not to archive every conversation, but to identify durable knowledge before it is lost.

## Preservation Criteria

Information shall be preserved whenever it:

- creates a reusable engineering solution;
- changes company philosophy;
- creates a new operating principle;
- establishes a customer-experience standard;
- documents a significant decision;
- improves operational efficiency;
- eliminates repeated future work;
- records an important lesson learned;
- defines company culture;
- improves communication;
- reduces future uncertainty.

## Information Classification

Every preserved item shall be classified. Possible destinations include:

- Operations Manual;
- Captain's Log;
- Bridge Logs;
- Engineering Standards;
- Research Archive;
- Customer Experience;
- Marketing;
- Product Strategy;
- Decision Register;
- Lessons Learned;
- Philosophy;
- Poetry;
- Future Missions.

## Knowledge Lifecycle

Knowledge follows this lifecycle:

1. Idea
2. Discussion
3. Decision
4. Implementation
5. Validation
6. Archive in The Book
7. Future Review

An item may be archived before validation when its unvalidated status is material and clearly labeled. The archive must never convert a hypothesis into a fact merely by preserving it.

## Review Policy

Commander Spock shall periodically review existing material. When information becomes obsolete, superseded, duplicated, or misleading, The Book shall be updated.

Historical decisions shall never be erased. They may instead be marked:

- **Superseded**
- **Deprecated**
- **Historical Context**

Git history should preserve prior states wherever practical.

## Slack Policy

Slack remains the operational workspace. It should contain:

- current discussions;
- active missions;
- temporary planning;
- daily reports;
- coordination.

Slack shall not become the permanent repository of company knowledge. Material with enduring value must be curated into The Book.

## Automatic Archival

Commander Spock is authorized to archive information without requesting permission whenever it clearly satisfies the preservation criteria.

Routine archival does not require Captain approval. Only structural changes to The Book itself require command review.

## Quality Principle

The Book is not a transcript, backup, or document dump. It is curated institutional memory.

Every entry should answer:

- What happened?
- Why did it matter?
- Why was this decision made?
- How should future officers use this knowledge?

## Guiding Maxims

> Every good choice begins with understanding.

> Observe → Understand → Improve.

> Future bridge crews inherit understanding—not archaeology.

> Continuity requires more than functioning code. It requires recoverable intent.

> Trust compounds.

> Knowledge compounds.

> Both require deliberate preservation.

## SO-004.1 — Stewardship

The crew shall leave USS Zupp1 more understandable at the end of each mission than it was at the beginning.

This stewardship applies not only to software and procedures, but to the company itself. Every completed mission should make Zupp1 clearer, wiser, and easier for future officers to operate.
