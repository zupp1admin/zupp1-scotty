### Assessment of the content-gap hypothesis
- 🟢 **Verified pattern:** For big "how to choose X" queries (sofa, closet), Brazil's SERPs are dominated by (1) major lifestyle-media portals (Globo properties: Casa Vogue, Casa e Jardim; Abril properties: CLAUDIA, CASA.COM.BR, CASACOR), (2) large marketplaces/retailers with dedicated content hubs (MadeiraMadeira's "Central de Dicas," Sun House, Sklum, eFácil, Westwing's "Guiar"), and (3) YouTube — not independent decision-assistance tools. This is consistent across every query tested.
- 🟡 **Inferred gap:** Almost none of the ranking content is *personalized* or *interactive* — it is static listicle/checklist content ("15 dicas," "7 passos"). None of the fetched pages offer an actual calculator (e.g., "enter your room's width and depth, get a recommended sofa size and model") or a structured decision tree that adapts to the user's stated constraints (budget, pets, children, room shape). This matches the pattern that made Casper's buying-guide content strategy notable internationally (see Section 2) — Brazilian retailers have replicated Casper's *content* tactic (blog posts targeting question keywords) but not yet its *interactive/personalization* tactic (the diagnostic quiz/survey funnel).
- 🟡 **Inferred gap:** Higher-intent, narrower questions (e.g., "sofá retrátil ou fixo," which combines product education with a binary decision) are already being actively targeted by smaller, newer niche sites in 2025-2026 (Sofá Review, Enfeite Decora, New Home Decorações), suggesting the "SEO arbitrage" opportunity in obvious head-terms is closing as competitors catch on — a decision-assistance platform's differentiation likely needs to be the *tool/interactivity*, not just more blog content, to stand out.
- 🔴 **No reliable data found** on exact monthly search volumes, keyword difficulty scores, or a verified ranked list of "low competition / high volume" queries. This requires a live SEMrush/Ubersuggest/Google Keyword Planner session tied to Zupp1's actual target keyword set.
- 🟢 **Verified adjacent signal:** Complaint volume data (Section 4/5) shows extremely high consumer pain around post-purchase regret and non-delivery in furniture — e.g., [Procon-SP registered 4,104 complaints against a single furniture retailer (Marabraz) in 2025](https://www.acidadeon.com/campinas/economia/clientes-da-marabraz-reclamam-de-atrasos-e-falta-de-reembolso-rede-lidera-queixas-no-procon/), and [complaints against "Sofá na Caixa" grew 126% in 2025 to 614 (Jan–Aug), with delivery delays as the top driver](https://g1.globo.com/tecnologia/noticia/2025/10/16/como-sofa-na-caixa-foi-de-sucesso-viral-nas-redes-a-explosao-de-reclamacoes-de-clientes.ghtml). This is not itself an SEO-content-gap finding, but it is strong corroborating evidence that consumers are making furniture purchase decisions with too little pre-purchase diagnostic information (wrong size/fit, unmet expectations) — a decision-assistance layer addresses this pain point independent of what current SEO tools show.

## 2. Existing "Decision-Assistance" Models: Store → Buying-Guide Repositioning

### International: Casper (mattresses) — the most-cited case
- 🟢 **Verified:** Casper built a content/SEO strategy around top-of-funnel informational content (its blog "Pillow Talk") ranking for broad sleep-related queries, a middle-of-funnel mattress-comparison resource, and — most relevant to "decision assistance" — **a sleep-habits survey/quiz** that visitors complete before being offered a $25 discount, rather than a simple email-capture form ([Reddit content-marketing case-study summary](https://www.reddit.com/r/content_marketing/comments/8q9flu/how_the_750_million_mattress_company_casper_does/)).
- 🟡 **Inferred why it worked:** The case-study explains three mechanisms: (1) answering top-of-mind audience questions built organic visibility/authority, (2) the survey captured first-party data segmented by real purchase-driving variables (sleep position, income, age), letting Casper personalize outreach, and (3) offering the discount *after* the diagnostic step increased motivation to convert without paying for the traffic. This is a structurally identical mechanism to what a "decision-assistance" furniture platform would want to replicate (diagnostic quiz → personalized recommendation → conversion).
- 🔴 No reliable data found on Casper's *quantified* uplift from this specific tactic (e.g., conversion-rate lift) — the source is a third-party marketing case study, not first-party Casper disclosure.

### Beauty: Sephora (Virtual Artist / Beauty Insider AI recommendations)
- 🟢 **Verified (via third-party case-study aggregator):** Sephora's "Virtual Artist" AI try-on tool reportedly drove purchasers to be **3x more likely to complete a purchase** than non-users, a **30% reduction in returns** for makeup products, and average app session time increasing from 3 to 12 minutes; the recommendation engine combines quiz data, browsing/purchase history, and product metadata via deep learning to personalize suggestions ([DigitalDefynd case study, 2026](https://digitaldefynd.com/IQ/sephora-using-ai-case-study/)).
- 🟡 Evidence strength caveat: this is a secondary aggregator source, not Sephora's own investor/press disclosure, so treat the specific percentages as **directionally indicative, not independently audited**. The pattern (AI-driven, quiz-informed personalization reducing returns and increasing basket confidence) is nonetheless a strong structural analogy for home-goods sizing/fit problems (which drive the CDC "right of withdrawal" returns discussed in Section 4).

### Brazil: Westwing — closest existing domestic analogue
- 🟢 **Verified (first-party source, investor relations page):** Westwing Brasil explicitly describes its business model as a **"shoppable magazine"** built on three pillars — curadoria (curation), freshness, and conteúdo (content) — and states that, unlike most ecommerce retailers who focus on "necessidade e busca" (need and search), Westwing's **primary strategy is discovery and inspiration**, delivered through in-house-produced editorial content and video, aimed at driving both engagement and conversion ([Westwing Brasil, "Quem Somos," Investor Relations](https://ri.westwing.com.br/westwing/quem-somos/)).
- 🟢 This is the strongest available Brazilian precedent for "store as content/guidance platform, not just transaction engine" — but it is positioned around **inspiration/discovery**, not explicitly around **decision assistance / reducing purchase uncertainty**. Zupp1's proposed positioning (helping people decide *what* to buy, i.e., resolving uncertainty and preventing regret) is adjacent to, but distinct from, Westwing's inspiration-led model. 🟡 Inferred: this suggests Zupp1 would be filling a different niche than Westwing (problem-solving/confidence vs. inspiration/discovery), reducing direct competitive overlap.
- 🔴 No reliable data found quantifying Westwing Brasil's content-driven conversion lift or ROI from this editorial strategy (searches returned the strategic description but not performance metrics tied specifically to content).

### MadeiraMadeira and other Brazilian retailers — content hubs, not yet "decision assistants"
- 🟢 **Verified:** MadeiraMadeira operates a "Central de Dicas" content hub with buying-guide articles (e.g., a detailed "como escolher o sofá ideal" guide, dated July 2026, covering room size, usage pattern, and sofa type) ([MadeiraMadeira Central de Dicas](https://www.madeiramadeira.com.br/central-de-dicas/artigos/como-escolher-sofa)). Similarly, eFácil ("Simplifica eFácil"), Sun House, and Sklum all publish comparable buying-guide blog content.
- 🟡 **Inferred:** This is evidence that **large Brazilian retailers already recognize content-driven guidance as valuable enough to invest in**, but the format observed across all of them is static blog content, not an interactive/personalized decision tool (quiz, calculator, AI chat). This is the specific gap a "decision-assistance platform" positioning could fill: moving from *content marketing* (which is already commoditized in this vertical) to *decision infrastructure* (personalized, interactive, AI-assisted).

### Summary judgment on Section 2
🟡 On the balance of evidence, "decision-assistance" repositioning has clear precedent and demonstrated upside in adjacent categories (mattresses, beauty) internationally, and Brazilian retailers are already moving toward content-led guidance (though not yet interactive/AI-personalized guidance) in home goods specifically. No Brazilian home-goods brand was found that has fully repositioned as a "decision assistant" rather than a store — this appears to be a genuine white space, not a saturated or previously-failed strategy. 🔴 No reliable data found on any Brazilian or international home-decor brand's *failed* attempt at this repositioning, so risk factors specific to failure modes remain unverified/must be evaluated independently by Zupp1.

## 4. Regulations & Consumer Protection Affecting Home-Goods Ecommerce in Brazil

### A. Right of withdrawal / 7-day return window (direito de arrependimento)
- 🟢 **Verified, primary source:** Article 49 of the Código de Defesa do Consumidor (Law 8,078/1990) grants consumers **7 calendar days from receipt of the product (or contract signature) to withdraw from any purchase made outside a physical commercial establishment** (explicitly including phone, catalog, and — by extension confirmed in secondary commentary — internet/ecommerce purchases), with **no justification required**, and **full, immediate, monetarily-updated refund** of all amounts paid, including shipping ([official consolidated text, Planalto/Presidência](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/l8078compilado.htm)).
- 🟢 **Verified:** A 2021 amendment (Lei 14.181/2021) added paragraphs clarifying that exercising this right also automatically voids any connected credit contract, and entitles the consumer to recover any taxes/charges paid via the credit intermediary ([same source](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/l8078compilado.htm)).
- 🟢 **Verified:** Refunds must include delivery/shipping fees, confirmed by consumer-advocacy source SPC Brasil ([SPC Brasil explainer](https://www.spcbrasil.com.br/blog/direito-de-arrependimento)).
- 🟡 **Practical implication for Zupp1:** Because this window is unconditional and justification-free, the CDC already gives Brazilian consumers a built-in "cooling-off" safety net for online furniture purchases — meaning the *legal* risk of a wrong purchase decision is capped, but the *practical/logistical* cost (large furniture is expensive/inconvenient to return; delivery delays discussed below compound this) is real and unaddressed by the law itself. This strengthens the case that "decision assistance" (getting it right the first time) has tangible value even though a legal remedy exists, because return logistics for large furniture are burdensome regardless of legal entitlement.

### B. Marketplace/platform joint liability (responsabilidade solidária)
- 🟢 **Verified, primary source:** A December 2025 Procon-MG/Ministério Público technical note confirms that Brazilian marketplaces are **strictly and jointly liable** (responsabilidade solidária) for defects/quality issues in products sold by third-party sellers on their platforms, grounded in CDC Articles 7 (parágrafo único), 12, 14, 18, 20, and 25 — liability that does **not** require proof of fault/negligence (responsabilidade objetiva) ([Nota Técnica n.º 05/2025, Procon-MG/MPMG, Dec. 19, 2025](https://www.mpmg.mp.br/data/files/FA/00/0B/DD/B483B91002A442B9B9A8F9C2/Nota%20Tecnica%20n%205_Responsabilidade%20solidaria%20de%20marketplaces%20por%20vicios%20e%20defeitos%20em%20produtos%20e%20servicos%20de%20parceiros_Procon-MPMG_19%20dez%202025.pdf)).
- 🟢 **Verified:** In June 2025, the Supremo Tribunal Federal (STF) ruled in RE 1.037.396 and RE 1.057.258 that Article 19 of the Marco Civil da Internet (which previously shielded platforms from liability absent a specific court order) does **not** apply irrestrictively, opening the door for CDC-based liability of marketplaces for third-party content/products — a significant 2025 jurisprudential shift ([Revista Eletrônica da OAB-RJ, June 2026](https://revistaeletronicaoabrj.emnuvens.com.br/revista/article/view/898)).
- 🟢 **Verified, sector-specific 2025 development:** Anatel updated its telecom-equipment homologation rules in 2025 to hold marketplaces (Mercado Livre, Amazon, Shopee, Magalu, etc.) solidarily liable for unhomologated/non-compliant products sold on their platforms, and mandated that listings display the Anatel homologation code ([ExpoEcomm, Aug 2025](https://www.expoecomm.com.br/anatel-passa-a-responsabilizar-marketplaces-por-produtos-irregulares-o-que-isso-significa-para-o-e-commerce)) — while this specific rule targets telecom devices, it signals a broader Brazilian regulatory trend (2025-2026) of **increasing marketplace accountability for what is sold on-platform**, relevant if Zupp1 operates or evolves toward a marketplace/multi-seller model.
- 🟢 **Verified, pending legislation:** PL 3024/2024 (Senate) proposes to formally codify marketplace joint liability specifically for counterfeit products sold directly or indirectly through ecommerce platforms — still in progress as of the note's registration date (Nov 2024) ([LexML/Senado Federal record](https://www.lexml.gov.br/urn/urn:lex:br:senado.federal:projeto.lei;pl:2024;3024)).

### C. Advertising / influencer marketing disclosure (CONAR)
- 🟢 **Verified, primary source:** CONAR's official "Guia de Publicidade por Influenciadores Digitais" requires that any influencer content meeting three cumulative criteria (product/brand promotion + any commercial/non-financial compensation + editorial control by the advertiser) must be **clearly and explicitly identified as advertising**, using standardized hashtags/terms — recommended terms include `#publicidade`, `#publi`, `#patrocinado`, `#publipost`, `#parceria paga`, `#ad`; explicitly **insufficient** are `#promo`, `#promoção`, mere brand tags, or coupon-code mentions alone ([CONAR Guide PDF](http://conar.org.br/pdf/CONAR_Guia-de-Publicidade-Influenciadores_2021-03-11.pdf)).
- 🟢 **Verified:** Disclosure must appear "in the first screen," be visible without requiring the viewer to click "see more," and — for time-limited content like Stories — must remain visible for the entire duration the content is available. Live-streams require periodic repeated disclosure.
- 🟢 **Verified:** The CONAR Guide is explicitly stated to operate as a **complement to** — not a replacement for — the CDC, the Marco Civil da Internet, the LGPD (Lei 13.709/2018), and Decrees 7.962/2013 and 10.271/2020 (Brazil's specific ecommerce consumer-protection decrees).
- 🔴 **No reliable data found** of CONAR guidance specific to home-decor/furniture influencer marketing (e.g., no home-decor-specific CONAR annex was located) — the general influencer rules apply without a sector carve-out. Home-decor influencer content (very common on Instagram/TikTok/Pinterest in Brazil) is subject to the same general disclosure rules as any other product category.

### D. Cross-border ecommerce / "Remessa Conforme" and import taxation (2024 developments — relevant to competitor dynamics, not directly to Zupp1's domestic operations unless it imports goods)
- 🟢 **Verified:** Since August 1, 2024 (Lei 14.902/2024, converting PL 914/2024), international purchases up to US$50 through platforms certified in the "Programa Remessa Conforme" (which include Mercado Livre, Magazine Luiza, Shein, Shopee, and others) are taxed at a **20% federal import duty** (previously 0%), plus a standard **17% state ICMS** applied on top — ending the prior full exemption ([Receita Federal official explainer](https://www.gov.br/receitafederal/pt-br/assuntos/aduana-e-comercio-exterior/manuais/remessas-postal-e-expressa/programa-remessa-conforme-o-que-e-como-funciona); [G1, July 2024](https://g1.globo.com/economia/noticia/2024/07/18/encomendas-internacionais-veja-empresas-que-estao-no-programa-da-receita-federal-e-as-que-buscam-habilitacao.ghtml); [Correios official tax tables](https://www.correios.com.br/central-de-informacoes/boletim-aos-clientes/novas-regras-declaracoes-de-importacoes-de-remessas)).
- 🟡 **Relevance to Zupp1:** This mainly affects small imported goods (apparel, gadgets) rather than large furniture (which is rarely cross-border-shipped economically), but it is a live 2024-2025 political flashpoint (there was a further 2025 proposal to raise the rate closer to 20-28%, per [InvestNews, Aug 2025](https://investnews.com.br/economia/governo-avalia-taxar-ao-menos-20-para-compra-de-ate-us-50-em-site-estrangeiro/)) that Zupp1 should monitor if any product lines involve cross-border sourcing or if Chinese fast-decor competitors (Shein Home, AliExpress home goods) become a competitive factor in Brazil.

### E. New tax-reform-era marketplace tax liability (LC 214/2025)
- 🟢 **Verified:** Brazil's 2025 tax-reform complementary law (LC 214/2025) introduces a new **joint tax liability regime for digital platforms** (Art. 22-24) for the new IBS/CBS consumption taxes — a marketplace can be held liable for a seller's unpaid IBS/CBS if the platform materially facilitated tax evasion (e.g., enabling under-invoicing), though **mere intermediation alone does not trigger liability** ([Fiscalia legal analysis, March 2026](https://fiscalia.app/blog/marketplace-responsabilidade-solidaria-reforma-tributaria); [Systax legal analysis, May 2026](https://www.systax.com.br/artigos-e-novidades/como-a-reforma-tributaria-muda-a-responsabilidade-de-marketplaces-e-plataformas-digitais/)). This is a 2025-2026 development Zupp1's finance/legal team should track as Brazil's broader tax reform continues rolling out.

### F. Sector-specific complaint/enforcement trend data (2025) — regulatory risk signal for the category
- 🟢 **Verified:** Procon-SP data shows furniture/planned-furniture ("móveis planejados") complaints **rose 10% year-over-year** (Jan-Jul 2025 vs. 2024), reaching 1,347 complaints in that period, with **non-delivery/delivery delay as the #1 complaint reason**, followed by incomplete/wrong/damaged product, then misleading offers/advertising ([G1, July 2025](https://g1.globo.com/sp/campinas-regiao/noticia/2025/07/26/clientes-denunciam-empresa-de-campinas-por-atraso-na-entrega-de-moveis-planejados.ghtml)).
- 🟢 **Verified:** A single furniture retailer (Marabraz) became the **#1 most-complained-about furniture company at Procon-SP in 2025 with 4,104 registered complaints** (2,544 for non-delivery/delay, 913 for refund difficulties) ([acidadeon/Campinas, Nov 2025](https://www.acidadeon.com/campinas/economia/clientes-da-marabraz-reclamam-de-atrasos-e-falta-de-reembolso-rede-lidera-queixas-no-procon/)).
- 🟢 **Verified:** "Sofá na Caixa," a direct-to-consumer sofa brand that grew through viral social media, saw Procon-SP complaints **grow 126% in 2025** (614 complaints Jan-Aug vs. 272 for all of 2024), almost entirely tied to delivery delay, and accumulated **7,861 complaints on Reclame Aqui**, prompting a formal Procon-SP investigation and possible inclusion on the "Evite Estes Sites" (avoid these sites) blacklist ([G1, Oct 2025](https://g1.globo.com/tecnologia/noticia/2025/10/16/como-sofa-na-caixa-foi-de-sucesso-viral-nas-redes-a-explosao-de-reclamacoes-de-clientes.ghtml); [Procon-SP official notice](https://www.procon.sp.gov.br/procon-sp-vai-fiscalizar-a-empresa-sofa-na-caixa/)).
- 🟢 **Verified, category-wide during peak sales events:** During Black Friday 2025, Procon-SP logged **2,979 formal complaints**, with non-delivery/delay (31.62%), unfulfilled cancellations (15.51%), and defective/wrong products (11.75%) as the top three causes across all ecommerce categories ([Procon-SP official release](https://www.procon.sp.gov.br/black-friday-2025-procon-sp-recebe-mais-de-3-mil-interacoes-e-encontra-irregularidades-em-mais-de-340-estabelecimentos-em-todo-o-estado/)).
- 🟡 **Strategic implication:** This data is not itself a new "regulation," but it is a strong regulatory-risk and reputational signal: **furniture ecommerce in Brazil has an outsized, well-documented, currently-worsening consumer-complaint profile**, concentrated on delivery/fulfillment failures and refund friction rather than on product-fit/decision-quality issues per se. A decision-assistance positioning does not directly fix delivery/logistics complaints, but it may reduce the "wrong choice → return/complaint" subset of this problem, and — more importantly for positioning — gives Zupp1 a credible public narrative of "we help you avoid regret" in a category where regulators, media, and Reclame Aqui rankings are actively spotlighting consumer distrust.

## 5. Pricing Intelligence: Transparency Tools for Brazilian Home Furniture

### Existing price-comparison/price-history tools
- 🟢 **Verified, primary source:** **Zoom** (zoom.com.br) is confirmed to operate a **"Histórico de Preços"** feature showing a product's price variation over 40 days, 3 months, 6 months, or 1 year, plus a **"Alerta de Preço"** (price alert) function notifying users by email/WhatsApp when a tracked product hits a target price; Zoom explicitly covers furniture ("móveis") among its tracked categories, alongside electronics and appliances ([Zoom official landing page](https://www.zoom.com.br/landing-page/historico-de-precos-zoom); [InfoPrice comparison of Brazilian price tools](https://www.infoprice.co/blog/6-sites-apps-para-comparar-precos/)).
- 🟢 **Verified, primary source:** **Buscapé** (buscape.com.br) offers an equivalent Histórico de Preços (same 40-day/3-month/6-month/1-year windows) and price-alert system; a Reclame Aqui thread confirms Buscapé's price-history depth was reduced from "up to 1 year" to 6 months following a technical integration with Zoom (the two platforms share ownership/backend), reducing functionality slightly as of that complaint's timeframe ([Buscapé official landing page](https://www.buscape.com.br/landing-page/historico-de-precos-buscape); [Reclame Aqui complaint thread confirming Zoom/Buscapé backend integration](https://www.reclameaqui.com.br/buscape/buscape-nao-mostra-mais-historico-de-precos-de-um-ano-atras-so-de-6-meses_4ggpKZiYsC3AanXZ/)).
- 🟢 **Verified:** Zoom explicitly states it vets/checks listed stores against Procon, Receita Federal, and Serasa records before allowing them to list, per a third-party comparison-tool roundup, and offers a "Zoom Garante" buyer-protection guarantee up to R$3,000 ([InfoPrice roundup](https://www.infoprice.co/blog/6-sites-apps-para-comparar-precos/); [TecMundo, legacy but consistent description](https://www.tecmundo.com.br/comercio-eletronico/69233-zoom-saiba-servico-detonando-outros-comparadores-precos.htm)).
- 🟡 **Assessment of transparency today:** Price-history/price-comparison infrastructure for furniture in Brazil is **more mature than commonly assumed** — Zoom and Buscapé both actively track furniture pricing with a "is this discount real" verification function explicitly marketed for events like Black Friday (countering "metade do dobro" — fake half-price — promotions). This is a genuinely useful consumer tool that already exists and is well-established (Zoom has operated since 2011). 🟡 However, 🔴 **no reliable data found** on furniture-category-specific coverage depth (i.e., what % of furniture SKUs sold by major Brazilian retailers are actually tracked with usable price history vs. showing no graph) — user complaints (Reclame Aqui, Buscapé's own help center) confirm that **not all products have price-history data available**, meaning coverage is inconsistent, especially likely for large-ticket, less-frequently-restocked furniture items compared to high-turnover electronics.
- 🟡 **Gap for Zupp1:** Neither Zoom nor Buscapé appears to combine price-history transparency with decision/fit guidance (i.e., they answer "is this a fair price" but not "is this the right product for me"). This reinforces the earlier finding (Sections 1-3) that the Brazilian market has reasonably good *price* transparency tools and increasingly good *content/inspiration* tools, but a gap remains in tools that combine price transparency with personalized product-fit decision-making — which is the specific union Zupp1's "decision-assistance platform" concept would occupy if built well.

### Regulatory backdrop relevant to pricing/discount advertising
- 🟢 **Verified:** A pending bill, PL 3019/2024, would classify as an *abusive practice* the advertising of medication discounts contingent on prior consumer registration/data capture — this is medication-specific and does **not** directly apply to furniture, but signals continued 2024-2025 legislative attention to discount-advertising practices in Brazilian ecommerce generally, worth monitoring for potential category extension ([Senado Federal, PL 3019/2024](https://www25.senado.leg.br/web/atividade/materias/-/materia/164835)).
- 🔴 **No reliable data found** of a Brazil-wide "minimum price over last 30 days" disclosure law (comparable to the EU's Omnibus Directive rule requiring retailers to show the lowest price offered in the preceding 30 days before a discount). No such general Brazilian statute was located in this search; Zoom/Buscapé's price-history tools currently substitute for this at the *platform* level rather than being a *legal requirement* imposed directly on individual retailers. Zupp1 should treat this as an open question rather than assume such a rule exists in Brazil today.

## Overall Assessment for Zupp1's "Decision-Assistance Platform" Hypothesis

The available evidence is **directionally supportive but not conclusively proven** for Zupp1's thesis:
- 🟢 There is a real, still-unfilled gap between (a) mature *price-transparency* tools (Zoom/Buscapé), (b) increasingly common but static *content/buying-guide* tools (MadeiraMadeira, eFácil, Sun House blogs), (c) niche *visualization* AI tools (Planner 5D, Homestyler, DecoraGPT), and (d) inspiration-led *curated commerce* (Westwing) — none of which combine into a single **personalized, interactive, purchase-decision-focused** experience.
- 🟢 Documented, worsening consumer pain (Procon-SP complaint growth, high-profile "Sofá na Caixa" and Marabraz cases) shows real market distrust concentrated on delivery/fulfillment more than on pre-purchase decision quality — meaning "decision assistance" alone will not resolve the sector's most visible complaint driver, and Zupp1 should not oversell this as a complete trust fix.
- 🔴 No reliable quantitative SEO/search-volume data was available in this session to size the specific content opportunity — this is the most significant remaining gap and should be closed with a live keyword-research tool session before investment decisions are finalized.
