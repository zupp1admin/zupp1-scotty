# Brazil Home-Buying Decision Behavior Research
### Market intelligence input for Zupp1 (Brazilian home-goods ecommerce)
Compiled August 2026. All claims are sourced with inline links to the fetched page. Evidence strength is noted for each data point.

---

## 1. SEARCH BEHAVIOR

**Headline finding: Brazilians research online before buying at rates well above the global average, and furniture/home is one of the categories where this is most pronounced.**

- According to PwC's **Global Consumer Insights Survey (GCIS)** — a large representative survey of **8,900+ consumers across 25 countries** — **79% of Brazilian consumers research before the final purchase decision**, versus a 56% global average. **63%** consult other customers' reviews before deciding (vs. 54% globally), and **65%** use retailer apps to research/compare products and prices (vs. 40% globally) ([PwC Brasil](https://www.pwc.com.br/pt/sala-de-imprensa/release/consumidor-inicia-escolha-por-produto-muito-antes-da-hora-da-compra.html); also reported by [Exame](https://exame.com/bussola/79-dos-brasileiros-pesquisam-antes-de-comprar-aponta-levantamento/)). *Evidence strength: large representative multi-country survey (n≈8,900), gold-standard for this claim.*

- An **IEMI** industry study, cited in a Sebrae-RS e-book on the furniture market, found that **63.6% of Brazilian furniture consumers research online before making a purchase** ([Sebrae RS e-book](https://conhecimento.sebraers.com.br/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/E-BOOK-MERCADO-MOBILIARIO-EXPERIENCIAS-E-COMPORTAMENTOS-DO-CONSUMIDOR-12608-.pdf)). The same source cites an **Opinion Box** finding that 83% of consumers say decor objects "must match my personal style" and 76% say the products they choose "say a lot about my personality" — signaling an emotionally driven, identity-linked search process, not purely price-driven. *Evidence strength: moderate — cites a named industry study (IEMI) and consumer-panel survey (Opinion Box), but full methodology not visible in this secondary source.*

- Opinion Box's dedicated **"Móveis e Decoração" survey (2021, n=2,100 Brazilians age 16+, all states, all social classes)** found that **58% of consumers search for furniture/decor products via search engines** and **56% search directly on the websites of stores they like/know** — meaning many shoppers skip generic search and go straight to trusted retailer sites ([UOL para Marcas](https://blog.publicidade.uol.com.br/insights/consumidor-de-moveis-e-decoracao-e-fiel-a-marcas-e-decide-por-preco-diz-pesquisa/)). The same survey found top-of-mind brand recall: Tok&Stok (10%), Etna (8%), Leroy Merlin (4%), Mobly (3%), MadeiraMadeira (2%) — two of the five being digital-first marketplaces. *Evidence strength: solid, named consumer panel with disclosed sample size and methodology.*

- A more recent (2026) study — **"Da busca à recomendação: como Google e IA influenciam as compras dos brasileiros"** by Rank Certo in partnership with Opinion Box (n=250) — found that "móveis e decoração" is cited by **41.2%** of respondents as a category where they research heavily before buying (behind electronics 75.2%, clothing/footwear 64.4%, cosmetics 42.4%). It also found **96.8%** of respondents trust Google's product suggestions, and **91.6%** have abandoned a purchase after finding a negative online review ([E-Commerce Brasil](https://www.ecommercebrasil.com.br/noticias/google-e-ia-influenciam-compras-mas-consumidor-ainda-compara-precos-mostra-pesquisa)). *Evidence strength: moderate — a real fielded survey but modest sample size (n=250), commissioned by an SEO agency (Rank Certo), so some promotional framing is likely.*

- General online pre-purchase research rates in Brazil are consistently reported as very high across several surveys: **97%** of Brazilian internet users research online before buying in physical stores per a CNDL/SPC Brasil study ([ABRAS/Abras clipping](https://www.abras.com.br/clipping/tecnologia/68431/97-dos-internautas-buscam-informacoes-online-antes-de-comprar-em-lojas-fisicas)); **9 in 10** Brazilians research before buying per Offerwise/Ipsos data cited by Google Brasil, with the average consumer visiting **6 different retail channels** and **75%** viewing 5+ pages before a Christmas purchase — the same report notes furniture and appliances see a search spike in January ([PEGN/Globo](https://revistapegn.globo.com/negocios/noticia/2023/03/nove-em-dez-brasileiros-pesquisam-online-antes-de-comprar-diz-google.ghtml)); and **93%** research on Google before buying per a 2022 e-commerce study ([E-Commerce Brasil](https://www.ecommercebrasil.com.br/noticias/pesquisa-google-antes-comprar)). *Evidence strength: mixed — these are industry/marketing surveys of varying rigor, but the directional consensus (very high pre-purchase research rates, 80–97% range) across five independent sources is strong.*

- **Google Trends** is confirmed as a viable free tool for tracking Brazilian search interest in home/furniture terms seasonally, filterable by region and category ([Google Trends Brasil](https://trends.google.com.br/explore)); no specific published Google Trends chart data on furniture keywords was found in secondary sources during this research — **Zupp1 should query Google Trends directly for "como escolher sofá," "guarda-roupa," "organização de casa," etc. as a next step; no reliable third-party republished dataset was found for these specific terms.**

- Practical/advice content site behavior: consumer guides emphasize measuring spaces, checking material/durability, and reading reviews as the standard "how to choose" (como escolher) research pattern for furniture — see Mobly's own buying guide, which recommends reading reviews first, verifying site security, then measuring the room and checking material specs before buying ([Mobly / Lar Style blog](https://www.mobly.com.br/blog/dicas/10-dicas-para-comprar-moveis/)) and Casa Vogue's "how to choose a sofa" guide covering model, size, material, color, and maintenance ([Casa Vogue](https://casavogue.globo.com/Interiores/Ambientes/noticia/2020/09/como-escolher-sofa-5-dicas-para-nao-se-arrepender-depois-da-compra.html)). *Evidence strength: low-to-moderate — these are retailer/lifestyle-media buying guides, not consumer-behavior surveys, but they reflect the "como escolher X" query pattern the task asked about.*

## 2. COMMON MISTAKES / REGRETS

**Headline finding: Buyer's remorse is very common in Brazil generally (>50%), and furniture-specific complaint data from Procon and Reclame Aqui is dominated by delivery delays, incomplete/incorrect assembly, and wrong/damaged products — not necessarily "wrong size/material" per se, though those are cited qualitatively by experts.**

### General buyer's remorse data
- An **Opinion Box survey (n=2,000+ Brazilians, purchases in the last 12 months)** found **63% of people who bought online said they regretted the purchase**, versus **54% for in-store purchases**. Of online buyers who regretted a purchase, **47% returned or exchanged the product** and 24% contacted the store to discuss options; **57%** said other consumers' opinions were "very important" to their purchase decision; and **22%** did not know they had a legal right of withdrawal (7-day "arrependimento" right under Brazil's Consumer Defense Code, Art. 49) ([Setor Moveleiro / Opinion Box](https://setormoveleiro.com.br/arrependimento-de-compra-setor-moveleiro/)). This article is furniture-industry-focused but explicitly notes the underlying survey was general (not furniture-specific); a furniture-sector expert quoted in the piece identifies **product defects, assembly problems, and delivery delays** as the typical drivers of furniture-specific regret, without providing a separate quantified breakdown. *Evidence strength: solid general survey (n=2,000+), but the furniture-specific attribution is qualitative/anecdotal, not separately measured.*

### Procon (consumer protection agency) complaint patterns — furniture specifically
- **Procon-SP** semi-annual furniture complaint data (São Paulo city, 2011–2013) shows a consistent ranking of complaint types: **product with defect** (2,009 complaints in 1H2013), **delivery delay or non-delivery** (1,801), **assembly delay/incorrect assembly** (544), **cancellation** (495), **product delivered different from order** (424), **warranty problems** (411), **incomplete delivery** (242) ([UOL Economia, citing Procon-SP data](https://economia.uol.com.br/financas-pessoais/noticias/redacao/2013/08/14/saiba-o-que-fazer-se-a-loja-atrasar-a-entrega-de-seus-moveis.htm)). *Evidence strength: solid — official Procon-SP statistics, though the data is from 2011–2013 and dated.*
- More recent: **Procon-SP** reported that furniture/custom-carpentry complaints in São Paulo rose **10%** in the first seven months of 2025 vs. 2024 (1,347 vs. 1,223 complaints through July 23), with **non-delivery/delayed delivery ranked #1**, **incomplete/wrong/damaged product ranked #2**, and **unfulfilled offers/misleading advertising ranked #3** ([G1](https://g1.globo.com/sp/campinas-regiao/noticia/2025/07/26/clientes-denunciam-empresa-de-campinas-por-atraso-na-entrega-de-moveis-planejados.ghtml)). *Evidence strength: solid, recent, official agency data (2025).*
- Local/state Procon data (Mato Grosso, 2023) shows delivery delay/non-delivery (280 complaints) and assembly delay (78 complaints) as the leading furniture-related complaint categories, broken down further by room type: bedroom furniture (50), kitchen furniture (22), living-room furniture (19) ([Jusbrasil, citing Procon-MT](https://www.jusbrasil.com.br/noticias/procon-orienta-sobre-prazo-para-entrega-de-produtos-e-demora-na-montagem/2041257)). *Evidence strength: moderate — official but regional/small-sample agency data.*

### Reclame Aqui (consumer complaint platform) — brand-specific evidence
- **Tok&Stok** complaints on Reclame Aqui **more than quadrupled** from **2,333 in 2024 to 9,924 in 2025**, and complaints in the first ~2 months of 2026 (2,201) were up **404%** year-over-year, driven by operational chaos from its 2024–2025 merger with Mobly (system integration failures, a backlog that peaked at 5,000 delayed orders out of ~150,000 monthly orders, and instances of customers receiving wrong products after multiple delivery failures) ([Diário do Comércio](https://diariodocomercio.com.br/negocios/tokstok-mobly-reclamacoes-clientes-quadruplicam-fusao/)). This is a acute, company-specific case but illustrates a systemic industry vulnerability: **logistics/delivery/assembly execution risk is the single biggest driver of furniture ecommerce complaints in Brazil**, capable of eroding a decades-old brand's reputation within months. *Evidence strength: solid — detailed investigative business journalism citing Reclame Aqui's own published complaint counts.*
- On Reclame Aqui's own live company pages, MadeiraMadeira shows **12,796 complaints** (Jan–Jun 2025 period) with an 85.1% resolution rate and 5.84/10 customer satisfaction score, and Tok&Stok shows **47,504 total complaints logged historically**, 92.7% resolution rate ([Reclame Aqui – MadeiraMadeira](https://www.reclameaqui.com.br/empresa/madeiramadeira/), [Reclame Aqui – Tok&Stok](https://www.reclameaqui.com.br/empresa/tokestok/lista-reclamacoes/)). *Evidence strength: solid — live platform data, though counts change continuously and are not furniture-only breakdowns (some Tok&Stok complaints may relate to non-furniture decor items).*
- General ecommerce complaint-type ranking (all categories, Black Friday 2024, Reclame Aqui): **delivery delay (17.41%)**, **misleading advertising (16.98%)**, **product not received (15.27%)**, **refund/chargeback issues (10.42%)**, **checkout problems (4.19%)** ([O Tempo](https://www.otempo.com.br/economia/2024/12/2/reclame-aqui-bate-recorde-historico-e-registra-14-mil-queixas-na)). *Evidence strength: solid — official Reclame Aqui data, but not furniture-specific.*

### Assessment on "wrong size / wrong material" specifically
No representative Brazilian survey or Procon/Reclame Aqui dataset was found that isolates **"wrong size" or "wrong material chosen"** as a discrete, quantified regret category (as opposed to the seller shipping the wrong product/material). Legal-advice content (Procon-RS consumer handbook) explicitly instructs consumers to refuse delivery and demand a refund **if the wood/material delivered doesn't match what was ordered** ("Se a madeira do móvel não for a mesma que você pediu, recuse a entrega") — this reflects a known failure mode (seller substitutes materials) rather than buyer measurement error ([Procon-RS consumer handbook](https://procon.rs.gov.br/upload/arquivos/201712/20172559-cartilha-do-consumidor-mj.pdf)). Multiple buying guides (Mobly, O Globo, Casa Vogue) explicitly warn shoppers to measure rooms and doorways before buying because "product photos don't always represent true size," implying this is a recognized (if unquantified) consumer pitfall ([Mobly](https://www.mobly.com.br/blog/dicas/10-dicas-para-comprar-moveis/); [O Globo](https://oglobo.globo.com/casa-e-conforto/quarto-como-escolher-os-principais-moveis-25013070)). **No reliable quantitative data found isolating consumer measurement/material-selection error as a % of complaints — this is a gap.**

## 3. TRUSTED INFORMATION SOURCES

**Headline finding: Search engines and store websites are the top research channels; but for validation/trust, other consumers' reviews, Reclame Aqui, and physical stores remain very influential, and social media (mainly Instagram) is a growing but secondary channel.**

- **Opinion Box's CX Trends 2025** (survey scale, part of its 10th annual edition; margin of error 2.2 pp on n=2,000+ per the related CX Trends 2025 wave) found the channels Brazilians consult before buying: **Google/search engines (56%)**, **physical stores (69%)**, **Reclame Aqui (49%)**, **product comment sections (48%)**, **WhatsApp (30%)**, **Instagram (29%)**, **TikTok (12%)**, **Facebook (11%)**. It also found **65% abandon a purchase due to high shipping cost**, the single biggest cart-abandonment driver ([Blog Opinion Box – CX Trends](https://blog.opinionbox.com/tendencias-de-customer-experience/)). *Evidence strength: solid — large, established annual tracking survey from a reputable Brazilian research firm.*

- The same **CX Trends 2025** research (Octadesk + Opinion Box, **n=2,000+ online consumers age 16+, all Brazilian states and social classes**) found: **68%** cite personalized service as a decision factor over the last 12 months; **50%** have had an AI experience while shopping (up 8 points YoY); **35%** experienced AI-personalized recommendations; **77%** shopped both online and in physical stores in the last year; top channels for choosing where to buy: free shipping (62%), product/service quality (56%), low price (53%); purchase channels: online stores (68%), marketplaces (66%), physical stores (64%), WhatsApp (30%), Instagram (28%) ([Inforchannel](https://inforchannel.com.br/2025/01/27/ia-e-hiperpersonalizacao-influenciam-6-em-cada-10-consumidores-no-brasil/)). *Evidence strength: solid — same large annual tracking study.*

- PwC's GCIS (n≈8,900, 25 countries) found Brazilian consumers' most-trusted research inputs, in descending order: **own research/product exploration (63% call it their top information source)**, customer reviews (63%), retailer apps (65%), in-store smartphone price comparison (46%), individual retailer websites (46%), social media as a research source (32%), word-of-mouth conversations (31%), price-comparison sites (21%) — with Brazil out-indexing the global average on almost every channel ([PwC Brasil](https://www.pwc.com.br/pt/sala-de-imprensa/release/consumidor-inicia-escolha-por-produto-muito-antes-da-hora-da-compra.html)). *Evidence strength: large representative multi-country survey.*

- For furniture/decor specifically, Opinion Box's 2021 "Móveis e Decoração" study found strong **brand loyalty and trust**: **51%** are loyal to a preferred furniture/decor store, **70%** are willing to pay more for a brand they like and trust, and price (44%), material quality (36%), durability (29%), promotions (25%), and shipping (21%) are the top purchase-decision factors ([UOL para Marcas / Henn Espaço Lojista](https://blog.publicidade.uol.com.br/insights/consumidor-de-moveis-e-decoracao-e-fiel-a-marcas-e-decide-por-preco-diz-pesquisa/); [Henn](https://lojista.henn.com.br/consumidor-de-moveis/)). This suggests that for home goods, **trusted retailer relationships and word-of-mouth/brand reputation outweigh generic comparison-shopping** relative to categories like electronics. *Evidence strength: moderate — single named survey, sample and methodology disclosed (n=2,100).*

- The 2026 Rank Certo/Opinion Box study found **64.4%** consider other buyers' opinions a relevant factor (more relevant than brand identity, cited by only 24.8%), and **91.6%** have abandoned a purchase after finding a negative review online — reinforcing that **peer reviews now outweigh brand marketing** in the Brazilian online purchase journey ([E-Commerce Brasil](https://www.ecommercebrasil.com.br/noticias/google-e-ia-influenciam-compras-mas-consumidor-ainda-compara-precos-mostra-pesquisa)). *Evidence strength: moderate (n=250, agency-commissioned).*

### How influential is influencer marketing for Brazilian consumers generally
- An **Opinion Box + Influency.me survey** ("Consumo e Influência Digital," sample size and dates not disclosed in this secondary source) found **7 in 10 Brazilian internet users follow digital influencers**, rising to **82% of women** and **91% of 16–29 year-olds**. Trust is the top factor for engagement (**59%** say content must be trustworthy). On purchase impact: **~65% of consumers have bought a product/service based on a creator's recommendation**, **87% were satisfied** with that purchase, and **80% would buy again** based on an influencer recommendation. YouTube (28%) and Instagram (27%) are the most purchase-influential platforms; TikTok is described as fast-growing among younger audiences without a specific percentage given ([Terra, citing Opinion Box/Influency.me](https://www.terra.com.br/noticias/sete-em-cada-dez-brasileiros-seguem-influencers-digitais,5d3e73b29a829d829b85d6b57993f68f0jdx2p7v.html)). *Evidence strength: moderate — a real named study, but sample size/methodology not disclosed in this article; figures are broad social-media/e-commerce statistics, not furniture/home-category-specific.*
- A related 2026 **Influency.me "Consumo e Influência Digital 2026" report** (developed with Opinion Box, **1,201 social-media users surveyed nationally**) found **84%** of respondents still value human-made content even with imperfections over AI-generated content, and **54%** disapprove of AI-generated product images — relevant context for any influencer/UGC content strategy Zupp1 might pursue ([Revista Empreende](https://revistaempreende.com.br/consumidores-desconfiam-de-imagens-geradas-por-ia/)). *Evidence strength: solid — disclosed sample size (n=1,201) though from an influencer-marketing company with commercial interest in these findings.*

### Category-specific influencer impact: no isolated data found
**No reliable data was found that isolates influencer-marketing effectiveness specifically for the furniture/home-organization/decor category** in Brazil (e.g., no study states "X% of furniture buyers were influenced by an Instagram/TikTok creator"). The figures above are either general cross-category influencer statistics or measure decor-influencer follower counts (reach), not attributed purchase conversion. **This is a data gap** — Zupp1 may want to commission or test this directly (e.g., via post-purchase attribution surveys) rather than relying on general benchmarks.

## 5. AI USAGE

**Headline finding: Brazil is one of the most AI-adopting consumer markets in the world, and there is now specific, credible data that AI already informs home/decor purchase decisions for a majority of Brazilians who use these tools.**

### General AI adoption in Brazil (context)
- **Bain & Company's "Consumer Pulse 2026"** report (global fieldwork, 8,000 total respondents, **2,000 in Brazil**) found **77% of Brazilians say they have used some form of AI**, up from **60–62% in 2025**. Critically, it found **60% of consumers are open to using AI for various types of purchases, including appliances/electronics (66%), home and decor (63%), and beauty/personal care (63%)** — this is the single most relevant statistic found for Zupp1's category ([Mobile Time, citing Bain](https://www.mobiletime.com.br/noticias/02/04/2026/inteligencia-artificial-2/); [Bain press release](https://www.bain.com/pt-br/about/media-center/press-releases/south-america/2026/77-dos-brasileiros-afirmam-utilizar-ia-aponta-relatorio-consumer-pulse-da-bain/)). *Evidence strength: solid — large, reputable global consulting-firm survey with a disclosed Brazil-specific sample (n=2,000).*

- **Adyen's 2025 Retail Report** found **52% of Brazilians used ChatGPT or other AI assistants to help with purchases in the last 12 months** (up from the prior year), with **18%** trying AI-assisted shopping for the first time in that period; adoption is highest among Gen Z (60%) and Millennials (62%) ([Adyen](https://www.adyen.com/pt_BR/imprensa-e-midia/2025-retail-report-ai)). *Evidence strength: solid — global payments company's proprietary consumer survey, Brazil-specific figures disclosed.*

- A **Nexus/FSB Holding survey** (in-person interviews, **n=2,012, all 27 Brazilian states, Aug 26–Sep 1, margin of error 2pp, 95% confidence**) found **37% of Brazilians have made at least one purchase decision influenced by AI responses** (ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Claude, etc.), rising to **46% among 18–30 year-olds**, 44% among those with higher education, 45% among those earning 5+ minimum wages, and 42% in the Southeast region. **63%** have used a generative AI tool at least once; **48%** use AI for general information search ([VEJA](https://veja.abril.com.br/coluna/planeta-ia/o-quanto-a-ia-ja-influencia-seu-consumo-mesmo-sem-voce-notar/); [Nexus](https://www.nexus.fsb.com.br/estudos-divulgados/51-dos-brasileiros-avaliam-que-ia-pode-tomar-decisoes-melhores-que-um-ser-humano-em-certas-situacoes/)). *Evidence strength: strong — rigorous methodology disclosed (sample size, dates, margin of error, confidence level), a rarity among the sources found.*

- **Opinion Box/Octadesk's "E-Commerce Trends 2026"** found **26% of Brazilian online shoppers already use tools like ChatGPT and Gemini to research products**, and another **26%** say they've been motivated to buy by AI-personalized product displays; only 39% feel AI improves the shopping experience overall, and 23% report distrust after learning a product description was AI-generated ([Meio e Mensagem](https://www.meioemensagem.com.br/marketing/ia-ja-impacta-consumo-e-maioria-compra-a-noite)). *Evidence strength: solid — large annual tracking survey (same Opinion Box panel referenced elsewhere, n=2,000+ in related waves).*

- **EY's Future Consumer Index** found **47% of Brazilians have not yet purchased based on an AI recommendation** (implying 53% roughly have, by inference, though EY frames it as the "not yet" statistic), while **84%** feel AI has improved their shopping experience and **87%** find AI product recommendations "highly useful" (+7pp YoY) ([EY](https://www.ey.com/pt_br/newsroom/2025/10/consumidores-compra-recomendada-ia)). *Evidence strength: solid — global professional-services firm's tracked index.*

### AI specifically for interior design / home decor use cases
- Multiple lifestyle-media pieces (Casa e Jardim, MRV's "Sonhar e Morar" blog, Olhar Digital) document a genuine consumer trend of Brazilians using **ChatGPT, Gemini, and dedicated apps like DecoraGPT** to redecorate rooms by uploading photos and requesting style transformations, layout suggestions, color palettes, and shopping lists — this is real, documented behavior at the how-to-guide level, though **not quantified by a survey** ([Casa e Jardim](https://revistacasaejardim.globo.com/tecnologia/noticia/2025/06/9-ferramentas-de-ia-para-decorar-ou-planejar-a-casa-de-maneira-facil.ghtml); [MRV Sonhar e Morar](https://sonharemorar.mrv.com.br/chatgpt-para-simular-decoracao/); [Olhar Digital](https://olhardigital.com.br/curiosidades/2026/07/03/6-formas-de-usar-o-chatgpt-para-projetar-a-planta-da-sua-casa-dos-sonhos-sem-pagar-arquiteto/)). *Evidence strength: low for quantification (these are "how-to" articles, not surveys) but useful as qualitative confirmation that the behavior exists and is mainstream enough to be covered by major outlets (Globo/Casa e Jardim).*
- The **Bain 63% figure for "casa e decoração"** (above) is the only quantified, named-source statistic found that directly measures AI openness for the home/decor category specifically — this should be treated as the most citable data point for Zupp1's specific use case.

### Generative AI traffic to Brazilian ecommerce (industry/traffic-analytics evidence)
- A **Cadastra + Similarweb** study (Jan 2023–Aug 2025, 600 sites, 12,000+ keywords tracked) found ChatGPT referral traffic to Brazilian e-commerce grew **71.9%** in the period studied, reaching **1.97 billion visits** in the Jan–Aug 2025 window; Brazil accounts for **67% of AI-tool traffic share** among LatAm markets studied, with **252.9 million average monthly visits**; the top 10 Brazilian e-commerce sites received **6.1 million visits from ChatGPT links** in the period, led by Mercado Livre (1.8M), OLX (914K), and Amazon (840.9K); Brazil is described as the **3rd largest market globally for generative-AI-tool traffic**, after the US and India ([E-Commerce Brasil](https://www.ecommercebrasil.com.br/noticias/ia-generativa-direciona-milhoes-de-visitas-ao-e-commerce-brasileiro-diz-estudo)). *Evidence strength: solid — named analytics vendor (Similarweb) methodology with disclosed timeframe and site count, though no furniture/home-specific breakout is given.*

**Conclusion for Section 5:** There is credible, converging data (Bain, Adyen, Nexus, EY, Opinion Box) that AI-assisted shopping is now mainstream in Brazil (roughly half or more of consumers have used AI in a shopping context, depending on definition), and one high-quality source (Bain, n=2,000 Brazil) explicitly quantifies **63% openness to AI use in the "home and decor" category** — a directly relevant, actionable data point for Zupp1.

## 6. E-COMMERCE CONTEXT

**Headline finding: Brazilian ecommerce overall is growing at roughly 10–17%/year and is worth R$200–260 billion annually; the "Casa e Construção"/"Móveis e Decoração" category is consistently one of the fastest-growing segments, cited at 17–27% annual growth in various 2025–2026 reports.**

### Overall Brazilian ecommerce size and growth
- **ABIACOM** (Associação Brasileira de Inteligência Artificial e E-commerce) reported Brazilian ecommerce closed **2025 at R$235.5 billion**, up **15.3%** YoY, with a **2026 projection of R$259.8 billion** (+10%), **460.87 million orders**, average ticket of **R$562.15**, and **97.06 million buyers** ([Dinheiro da Minha Empresa, citing ABIACOM/ABComm](https://dinheirodaminhaempresa.com/onclick/indice-varejo-digital-brasil-2026/)). ABComm separately projects continued ~10%/year growth through 2029, reaching **R$343 billion** ([Times Brasil/CNBC](https://timesbrasil.com.br/empresas-e-negocios/tecnologia-e-inovacao/e-commerce-no-brasil-passa-de-r-200-bilhoes-e-vira-mainstream-com-ia-whatsapp-e-mobile/)). *Evidence strength: solid — figures from Brazil's main ecommerce trade association, cross-confirmed by multiple outlets.*
- **H1 2026**: Brazilian ecommerce revenue reached **R$208.4 billion**, up **16.9%** YoY, with **756.7 million orders** (+34.8%). Within this, **"Casa e Construção" (Home & Construction) grew 26.9%** — one of the fastest-growing named categories, behind only Fashion/Accessories (23.1%) trailing Health (45.8%) ([E-Commerce Brasil](https://www.ecommercebrasil.com.br/noticias/e-commerce-brasileiro-fatura-r-2084-bilhoes-no-primeiro-semestre)). *Evidence strength: solid — recent (2026), named trade-press report.*
- **Black Friday 2025** (Nov 1–24): Brazilian digital sales totaled **R$33.6 billion**, up **35.5%** YoY; **"Casa e Construção" grew 42.2%**, attributed to a home-renovation cycle ([TI Inside, citing Neotrust/Confi](https://tiinside.com.br/28/11/2025/faturamento-do-e-commerce-na-black-friday-sera-17-superior-a-2024-aponta-confi-neotrust/)). *Evidence strength: solid — Neotrust/Confi is a leading Brazilian ecommerce data provider.*

### Furniture & home/decor category specifically
- **ABComm and Neotrust** consolidated data show the **"móveis e decoração" (furniture and decor) category grew 17% in Brazilian ecommerce in 2025**, and small/medium niche home brands (without outside investors) are growing **2–3x faster than the category average** ([Valor/Globo, sponsored content citing ABComm/Neotrust data](https://valor.globo.com/patrocinado/dino/noticia/2026/07/16/marcas-brasileiras-ganham-espaco-no-ecommerce-nacional-1.ghtml); [São Paulo Aberta](https://saopauloaberta.com.br/casa/o-lar-virou-prioridade-de-consumo-no-brasil-e-o-e-commerce-de-decoracao-se-reorganiza-em-torno-do-nicho/)). The same source cites **Abcasa** data that the Brazilian home-goods/decor market moved **~R$45 billion in 2024**, projected to reach **R$60 billion by 2027**, with the category growing at roughly **8% per year** and ranking **3rd in total ecommerce sales** nationally. It also cites **Neotrust/Ebit-Nielsen** data that the category's online **conversion rate is only ~1.6%** — one of the lowest in ecommerce, attributed to consumers visiting multiple times, comparing, and taking longer to decide (directly relevant to Zupp1's UX/content strategy). Bed/bath sales specifically grew **>20%** in 2025. *Evidence strength: moderate — this is aggregated/paraphrased secondary reporting rather than a direct link to the original ABComm/Neotrust/Abcasa reports, but the reporting outlet (Valor/Globo) is a top-tier Brazilian business publication and the figures are internally consistent with other sources below.*

- **ECDB (Ecommerce DB)** market-intelligence data: the Brazilian **Furniture & Homeware ecommerce market is projected to reach US$4,583.9 million by 2025** (10.3% of total Brazilian ecommerce), growing at a **9.0% CAGR (2025–2029)** to reach **US$6,463.1 million by 2029**; within this, **"Homeware" alone is projected at US$2,033.0 million by 2025** (44.4% of the Furniture & Homeware total), growing at 5.9% CAGR to **US$2,558.4 million by 2029** ([ECDB](https://ecommercedb.com/markets/br/homeware)). A related ECDB furniture-specific page states the furniture ecommerce market generated **US$3,569 million in 2025 revenue**, growing **10–15% YoY**, with **"Home & Living" the largest sub-category at 68% of furniture ecommerce revenue**, and Mercado Livre as the largest single retailer (US$65,037m total online revenue in 2025, across all categories) ([ECDB Furniture](https://ecdb.com/resources/sample-data/market/br/furniture)). *Evidence strength: solid — specialized ecommerce market-intelligence vendor with modeled but methodologically transparent estimates; note these are commercial market-research estimates, not government/trade-association hard figures, so treat as directional.*

- **2020–2021 pandemic-era baseline** (for historical context): Ebit/Nielsen's Webshoppers report found the "Casa e Decoração" category grew **71% in order volume in 2020** (representing 15% of total ecommerce orders, 12% of revenue) ([CNN Brasil, citing Webshoppers 43](https://www.cnnbrasil.com.br/economia/macroeconomia/com-a-maior-alta-em-13-anos-e-commerce-sobe-41-em-2020-e-atinge-r-87-4-mi/)); by H1 2021, the category's growth accelerated further to **+155% in sales value** and **+67% in order volume**, with **34% of category traffic coming from search engines and 20% from social media** — the highest search-engine share of any category studied except automotive ([Móveis de Valor, citing Ebit|Nielsen](https://www.moveisdevalor.com.br/noticia/e-commerce-de-casa-e-decoracao-registra-alta-de-155-em-vendas)). *Evidence strength: solid — named Ebit|Nielsen Webshoppers data, though now dated (2020–2021 pandemic-boom period, not representative of current growth rates).*

- **Global/Latin American context**: Ken Research estimates the **Brazil furniture market at USD 18.7–19.4 billion** (varying by source/year) with Brazil as the **largest furniture market in Latin America** (~USD 15.8 billion in demand, 2025), within a **USD 36.9 billion Latin America furniture market** growing at a 5.2% CAGR through 2031 ([Ken Research — Brazil](https://www.kenresearch.com/brazil-furniture-market), [Ken Research — LatAm](https://www.kenresearch.com/industry-reports/latin-america-furniture-market)). Mordor Intelligence separately estimates the Brazil furniture market at **USD 16.15 billion (2026)**, forecast to reach **USD 19.74 billion by 2031** (4.1% CAGR), with **home furniture representing 55% of the market** by application ([Mordor Intelligence](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/brazil-furniture-market)). *Evidence strength: moderate — these are paid market-research firms; note the noticeable divergence between estimates (16.1–19.4 billion range for similar years) reflects differing methodologies; treat as order-of-magnitude estimates rather than precise figures.*

- **Marketplace dominance**: roughly **80% of Brazilian online sales now flow through marketplaces** (Mercado Livre, Amazon, Shopee, Magalu, etc.) rather than direct-to-consumer sites, a trend expected to continue in 2026 ([Dinheiro da Minha Empresa](https://dinheirodaminhaempresa.com/onclick/indice-varejo-digital-brasil-2026/)) — an important structural consideration for Zupp1's channel strategy.
