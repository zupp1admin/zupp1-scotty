## 1. How do Brazilians decide what to buy for their homes?

**FACT.** Brazilians research more before buying than almost any other market measured. PwC's Global Consumer Insights Survey (n≈8,900 across 25 countries) found **79% of Brazilians research before a final purchase decision**, versus a 56% global average; 63% consult other customers' reviews and 65% use retailer apps to compare prices, both above the global average ([PwC Brasil](https://www.pwc.com.br/pt/sala-de-imprensa/release/consumidor-inicia-escolha-por-produto-muito-antes-da-hora-da-compra.html)). For furniture specifically, an IEMI industry study cited by Sebrae-RS found **63.6% of furniture buyers research online before purchasing** ([Sebrae-RS](https://conhecimento.sebraers.com.br/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/E-BOOK-MERCADO-MOBILIARIO-EXPERIENCIAS-E-COMPORTAMENTOS-DO-CONSUMIDOR-12608-.pdf)), and Opinion Box's 2021 "Móveis e Decoração" study (n=2,100) found 58% search via search engines and 56% go directly to a retailer they already trust ([UOL para Marcas](https://blog.publicidade.uol.com.br/insights/consumidor-de-moveis-e-decoracao-e-fiel-a-marcas-e-decide-por-preco-diz-pesquisa/)).

**FACT.** Furniture/decor purchasing is identity-driven, not just price-driven: 83% say decor objects "must match my personal style" and 76% say their choices "say a lot about my personality" ([Sebrae-RS](https://conhecimento.sebraers.com.br/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/E-BOOK-MERCADO-MOBILIARIO-EXPERIENCIAS-E-COMPORTAMENTOS-DO-CONSUMIDOR-12608-.pdf)). Brand loyalty in this category is unusually high: 51% are loyal to a preferred furniture/decor retailer and 70% will pay more for a brand they trust ([Henn](https://lojista.henn.com.br/consumidor-de-moveis/)).

**FACT.** The category's online conversion rate is only about **1.6%** — one of the lowest in Brazilian ecommerce — attributed to repeated visits, comparison, and slow decision-making ([Valor/Globo](https://valor.globo.com/patrocinado/dino/noticia/2026/07/16/marcas-brasileiras-ganham-espaco-no-ecommerce-nacional-1.ghtml)). Meanwhile the category itself is growing fast: "móveis e decoração" grew 17% in Brazilian ecommerce in 2025, "Casa e Construção" grew 26.9% in H1 2026, and Black Friday 2025 home-category sales rose 42.2% ([ABComm/Neotrust via Valor/Globo](https://valor.globo.com/patrocinado/dino/noticia/2026/07/16/marcas-brasileiras-ganham-espaco-no-ecommerce-nacional-1.ghtml); [E-Commerce Brasil](https://www.ecommercebrasil.com.br/noticias/e-commerce-brasileiro-fatura-r-2084-bilhoes-no-primeiro-semestre); [TI Inside](https://tiinside.com.br/28/11/2025/faturamento-do-e-commerce-na-black-friday-sera-17-superior-a-2024-aponta-confi-neotrust/)).

**ANALYSIS.** These facts together describe a long, multi-touch, emotionally loaded decision journey — not an impulse category. A shopper researches across 5+ retail channels, values identity/style fit almost as much as price, defaults to brands they already trust, and still converts at a very low rate. That combination — high research intensity + low conversion + identity stakes — is exactly the profile of a category where a *decision layer* (something that shortens or de-risks the "is this the right one for me" step) has room to add value, independent of price competition. *Confidence: High* — this conclusion rests on convergent, large-sample, methodologically disclosed surveys (PwC n=8,900; Opinion Box n=2,100+).

## 2. What mistakes do they make most often?

**FACT.** 63% of Brazilians who bought online in the last year reported regretting the purchase, versus 54% for in-store purchases ([Opinion Box, via Setor Moveleiro](https://setormoveleiro.com.br/arrependimento-de-compra-setor-moveleiro/)). For furniture specifically, official Procon data is dominated by **operational, not decision, failures**: product defects, delivery delay/non-delivery, incorrect/delayed assembly, wrong or incomplete deliveries, and misleading offers, consistently ranked in that order across Procon-SP data from 2013 and again in 2025 (1,347 furniture complaints Jan–Jul 2025, up 10% YoY, with non-delivery ranked #1) ([UOL/Procon-SP](https://economia.uol.com.br/financas-pessoais/noticias/redacao/2013/08/14/saiba-o-que-fazer-se-a-loja-atrasar-a-entrega-de-seus-moveis.htm); [G1](https://g1.globo.com/sp/campinas-regiao/noticia/2025/07/26/clientes-denunciam-empresa-de-campinas-por-atraso-na-entrega-de-moveis-planejados.ghtml)). Named-brand evidence reinforces this: Marabraz logged 4,104 Procon-SP complaints in 2025, mostly delivery-related ([acidadeon](https://www.acidadeon.com/campinas/economia/clientes-da-marabraz-reclamam-de-atrasos-e-falta-de-reembolso-rede-lidera-queixas-no-procon/)); "Sofá na Caixa" complaints rose 126% in 2025 ([G1](https://g1.globo.com/tecnologia/noticia/2025/10/16/como-sofa-na-caixa-foi-de-sucesso-viral-nas-redes-a-explosao-de-reclamacoes-de-clientes.ghtml)); Tok&Stok's Reclame Aqui complaints quadrupled (2,333 → 9,924) in 2025 amid its Mobly-merger integration failure ([Diário do Comércio](https://diariodocomercio.com.br/negocios/tokstok-mobly-reclamacoes-clientes-quadruplicam-fusao/)).

**FACT.** 22% of regretful online buyers didn't know they had a legal right to a no-questions-asked 7-day return ([Opinion Box, via Setor Moveleiro](https://setormoveleiro.com.br/arrependimento-de-compra-setor-moveleiro/)).

**INTELLIGENCE GAP.** No Procon, Reclame Aqui, or survey dataset found in this research isolates "consumer chose the wrong size / wrong material" as its own quantified category. Buying guides (Mobly, Casa Vogue, O Globo) routinely warn shoppers to measure rooms and doorways because "photos don't always represent true size," which implies this is a known failure mode — but it has never been separately measured ([Mobly](https://www.mobly.com.br/blog/dicas/10-dicas-para-comprar-moveis/); [O Globo](https://oglobo.globo.com/casa-e-conforto/quarto-como-escolher-os-principais-moveis-25013070)). *Confidence: Low* on this specific sub-question — we genuinely do not know what share of furniture regret is caused by the buyer's own decision error versus seller/operational failure.

**ANALYSIS.** The dominant, well-documented "mistake" in Brazilian furniture ecommerce today is not a *decision* mistake at all — it's a *fulfillment* failure (late, wrong, damaged, or unassembled deliveries). This matters directly for Zupp1's positioning claim. *Confidence: High* on the fulfillment-dominance finding (multiple official Procon/Reclame Aqui datasets converge); *Low* on how large the decision-error subset actually is, because nobody appears to measure it.

**STRATEGIC OPINION.** If "helping people decide well" is offered as the antidote to the sector's visible complaint problem, the evidence says that's only a partial fix — the biggest measured pain point is logistics, which decision assistance does not touch. This is the kind of gap between "the story we tell" and "the evidence" that Standing Order 6 exists to flag: **the current Zupp1 narrative should be checked against this data before it's stated publicly as "we fix the #1 problem in this category."** It doesn't — it addresses a real but currently unmeasured second-order problem.

## 3. What information do they trust?

**FACT.** Opinion Box's CX Trends 2025 (n=2,000+, annual tracking study) found Brazilians consult, before buying: physical stores (69%), search engines (56%), Reclame Aqui (49%), product comment/review sections (48%), WhatsApp (30%), Instagram (29%), TikTok (12%), Facebook (11%) ([Opinion Box](https://blog.opinionbox.com/tendencias-de-customer-experience/)). PwC's GCIS ranks Brazilians' top trusted inputs as their own research, customer reviews, and retailer apps, all above social media or price-comparison sites as trust sources ([PwC Brasil](https://www.pwc.com.br/pt/sala-de-imprensa/release/consumidor-inicia-escolha-por-produto-muito-antes-da-hora-da-compra.html)). A 2026 Rank Certo/Opinion Box study found 64.4% weight other buyers' opinions as a relevant factor versus only 24.8% for brand identity, and 91.6% have abandoned a purchase after finding a negative review ([E-Commerce Brasil](https://www.ecommercebrasil.com.br/noticias/google-e-ia-influenciam-compras-mas-consumidor-ainda-compara-precos-mostra-pesquisa)).

**FACT.** Brazil has a mature, monetized home-decor/organization "cleanfluencer" ecosystem (Karla Amadori, Rafaela Oliveira/Organize Sem Frescuras, Maddu Magalhães, and cleaning creators earning up to R$30–40k/post) ([Influency.me](https://influency.me/blog/os-melhores-influenciadores-de-casa-e-decoracao-2/); [Terra](https://www.terra.com.br/economia/cleanfluencers-brasileiros-mostram-dia-a-dia-de-faxinas-pesadas-e-ganham-ate-r-30-mil-por-post,b54a9bf9f88d1ae16d4c45550e72f444hdxk280z.html)). Generally, ~65% of Brazilians have bought something on an influencer's recommendation and 87% were satisfied ([Terra, citing Opinion Box/Influency.me](https://www.terra.com.br/noticias/sete-em-cada-dez-brasileiros-seguem-influencers-digitais,5d3e73b29a829d829b85d6b57993f68f0jdx2p7v.html)) — but **no data isolates this effect for the furniture/home category specifically**. That is an Intelligence Gap, not a confirmed furniture-category statistic.

**FACT.** AI is a fast-rising trust channel specifically for this category: Bain's Consumer Pulse 2026 (n=2,000 Brazil) found **63% of Brazilians are open to using AI for "casa e decoração" purchases** ([Bain, via Mobile Time](https://www.mobiletime.com.br/noticias/02/04/2026/inteligencia-artificial-2/)), and Nexus/FSB (n=2,012, rigorous disclosed methodology) found 37% have already made a purchase decision influenced by generative AI ([Nexus, via VEJA](https://veja.abril.com.br/coluna/planeta-ia/o-quanto-a-ia-ja-influencia-seu-consumo-mesmo-sem-voce-notar/)).

**ANALYSIS.** The trust hierarchy for Brazilian home purchases is: peer evidence (reviews, Reclame Aqui) and personal/owned research first, physical validation second, brand relationships third, and social/AI channels fourth but rising fast. Brazilians trust *evidence of other people's real experience* over marketing messaging. *Confidence: High* on the general hierarchy (multiple large, independent, converging surveys); *Medium* on how this maps specifically onto furniture (most granular data is a single 2021 survey); *Low* on isolating AI or influencer trust specifically within this category (only one usable data point each — Bain's 63% and no isolated influencer figure at all).

**CONFIDENCE SIGNAL.** The convergence of high peer-trust weighting and rising AI-openness specifically for "casa e decoração" is consistent with — and supports — a platform built on transparent, evidence-synthesizing content rather than inspiration or brand marketing. This is one of the stronger pieces of evidence in this mission that Zupp1's evidence-first posture is aimed at the right trust signal.

## 4. What information is missing?

**INTELLIGENCE GAP #1 — No live search-volume data.** Google Trends blocked direct access and no SEMrush/Ubersuggest session was available in this research pass. We have a qualitative content-landscape audit (what ranks today for queries like "como escolher sofá ideal," "medidas ideais para sofá sala pequena," "como organizar closet pequeno") but **no verified keyword volume or difficulty numbers**. This is a hard limitation, not a soft one — Zupp1 should not commit to a content roadmap sized on assumption. *Confidence in the underlying qualitative pattern (static, non-interactive content dominates rankings): Medium. Confidence in any volume estimate: none — do not use one until it's pulled live.*

**INTELLIGENCE GAP #2 — No quantified furniture-specific "decision mistake" rate.** As noted in Section 2.

**INTELLIGENCE GAP #3 — No furniture/home-category-isolated influencer conversion data.** As noted in Section 3.

**FACT.** What we *can* verify: existing AI decor tools in the Brazilian market (Planner 5D, Homestyler, IKEA Place, DecoraGPT, and several 2025–2026 entrants with BRL-localized pricing) solve **visualization** ("what will this look like in my room") but none function as a **purchase-decision/comparison assistant** ("what should I buy, at what price, and is it right for my situation") ([Casa e Jardim/Globo](https://revistacasaejardim.globo.com/tecnologia/noticia/2025/06/9-ferramentas-de-ia-para-decorar-ou-planejar-a-casa-de-maneira-facil.ghtml)). Planner 5D, the most visible of these, ranks only #42 in Brazil's Lifestyle app category — real but niche usage ([Similarweb](https://www.similarweb.com/app/apple/606173978/brazil/)).

**FACT.** Existing price-transparency tools (Zoom, Buscapé) are mature, cover furniture, and actively flag fake "metade do dobro" discounts — but neither combines price history with personalized fit/decision guidance, and SKU coverage is inconsistent for large-ticket furniture ([Zoom](https://www.zoom.com.br/landing-page/historico-de-precos-zoom); [Buscapé](https://www.buscape.com.br/landing-page/historico-de-precos-buscape)).

**ANALYSIS.** Putting Sections 4's findings together: Brazil has mature price-transparency tools, increasingly common static buying-guide content, and a growing set of visualization AI tools — but **no single tool combines price transparency + personalized fit guidance + interactivity** for home goods. *Confidence: Medium.* This is an absence-of-evidence finding across the searches conducted in this mission, not an exhaustive, independently-verified market scan (no Similarweb Pro or SEMrush account was used to confirm zero traffic for smaller niche players). Treat the "white space" as a working hypothesis to validate further, not a settled fact.

## 5. Which competitors are best helping consumers make better home decisions?

**COMPETITIVE BRIEF — Magazine Luiza.** Magalu is doing something no other major Brazilian marketplace is currently doing at scale: it operates "Lu," a generative-AI shopping assistant (Luizalabs + Google Vertex AI, launched 2023) and, since November 2025, "WhatsApp da Lu" — a full AI-driven conversational commerce flow (search, personalized recommendation, Pix/card payment, post-sale support) built with Meta, Google, and Bain & Company, covering Magalu's 1P catalog plus 300,000+ marketplace sellers and 37M+ listings. Per Magalu's own investor-relations disclosures, it generated **over 9 million conversations in its first six months, with 3x the conversion rate of other channels** ([Magalu IR, Nov 2025](https://ri.magazineluiza.com.br/List/Download.aspx?Arquivo=8lqkxA0eHuthoyoNG0YM0w==); [Magalu IR, June 2026](https://ri.magazineluiza.com.br/List/Download.aspx?Arquivo=8lqkxA0eHuthoyoNG0YM0w==); corroborated by [O Globo](https://oglobo.globo.com/economia/negocios/noticia/2025/11/05/ai-commerce-magalu-comeca-a-operar-ferramenta-de-compras-com-inteligencia-artificial-veja-como-funciona.ghtml)). *Confidence: High — this is company-disclosed, investor-grade data, not marketing copy.*

**FACT.** MadeiraMadeira is the furniture-vertical leader in buying-guide content specifically: its "Central de Dicas" content hub carries dedicated guides (e.g., "Melhores guarda-roupas de 2024," "como escolher o sofá ideal") plus physical "Guide Shop" showrooms, and its CEO states roughly 30% share of the Brazilian online furniture category ([MadeiraMadeira](https://www.madeiramadeira.com.br/central-de-dicas); [Exame](https://exame.com/negocios/madeiramadeira-cashback-ipo-vendas-produtos/)). Mercado Livre, the largest platform by both GMV (~R$139bn, ~47% of domestic GMV) and traffic, runs an active decor blog vertical but **no AI-driven, buyer-facing decision tool was found** ([Mercado Livre blog](https://www.mercadolivre.com.br/blog/moveis-e-decoracao/1); [YAV](https://yav.com.br/blog/maiores-empresas-ecommerce-brasil/)).

**FACT.** Amazon.com.br and Shopee Brasil — despite meaningful scale (9.7% and 12.5% traffic share respectively) — show **no evidence of any dedicated home-decor content, buying guide, or AI decision tool**; both rely on generic global Q&A/review features only ([Conversion](https://www.conversion.com.br/blog/ranking-ecommerces)). This is a clear, sourced relative gap for two of Brazil's largest platforms.

**FACT.** Smaller, dedicated decision/comparison players exist — Mybest Brazil (cross-category "best of" rankings synthesizing Reclame Aqui and Amazon review scores), Ordenato (multi-marketplace curation blog since 2015), SofasExpres and Sofá Review (single-category sofa comparison sites), Méliuz (cashback + buying guides), Westwing Brasil (inspiration-led curated retail, not decision-led) — but **none were verified at meaningful scale via independent traffic data** ([Mybest](https://br.my-best.com/18473); [Ordenato](https://ordenato.com.br/quem-somos/); [SofasExpres](https://sofasexpres.com/); [Westwing IR](https://ri.westwing.com.br/westwing/quem-somos/)). *Confidence: Low-Medium on "no one at scale exists" — this is an absence-of-evidence conclusion, not an independently audited competitive census.*

**ANALYSIS.** Magazine Luiza is the benchmark to study, not to compete against head-on — it has scale, capital, and Big Tech partnerships Zupp1 does not have. The more defensible gap is the **cross-marketplace, marketplace-agnostic decision layer**: none of the big five, and none of the smaller niche players found, combine price comparison across sellers + personalized fit guidance + interactivity in one place for home goods.

## 6. Is there evidence a "decision-assistance platform" solves a real problem?

**FACT recap:** 79–97% pre-purchase research rate; ~1.6% category conversion rate; 63% online buyer's-remorse rate; rising Procon/Reclame Aqui complaint volumes in furniture; 63% openness to AI specifically for "casa e decoração"; no existing Brazilian tool combines price transparency, fit guidance, and interactivity; Westwing Brasil proves a large content-led home business is buildable in Brazil, though on an inspiration (not decision) thesis; Casper and Sephora show quantified upside internationally from diagnostic/personalized mechanisms (3x purchase likelihood, 30% fewer returns for Sephora's Virtual Artist — via a secondary case-study aggregator, not Sephora's own audited disclosure) ([DigitalDefynd](https://digitaldefynd.com/IQ/sephora-using-ai-case-study/)).

**CONFIDENCE SIGNAL.** These threads converge in one direction — a long, friction-heavy, trust-sensitive decision process with no existing tool addressing the full problem. *Confidence: Medium-High* that a real, unmet decision-support need exists in this category in Brazil.

**ANALYSIS — the honest caveat.** No source in this research directly measures the *effect* of a "decision-assistance platform," as a concept, on conversion, satisfaction, or return rates in the Brazilian home-goods category. The supporting evidence is indirect: general research/regret statistics, adjacent-category international case studies (mattresses, beauty — not audited, secondary sources), and the absence of a scaled Brazilian competitor doing exactly this. That is a real, converging signal — but it is not a controlled or causal proof. *Confidence: Low* that "decision-assistance" specifically (versus generic content marketing, better logistics, or lower prices) is *the* lever that moves the needle here — nobody has tested that variable in isolation.

**STRATEGIC OPINION.** The honest answer to Question 6 is: **the market problem this positioning targets is real and well evidenced; the specific solution ("decision-assistance platform" as a business model) is a well-supported hypothesis, not a proven one.** That distinction matters for how confidently this gets stated to the team or to customers.

## 7. If Zupp1 continues on its current trajectory, what are the three biggest opportunities?

**OPPORTUNITY BRIEF 1 — Own the combined decision layer.** No competitor found (major marketplace or niche site) combines price transparency + personalized fit/size guidance + interactivity for home goods. *Confidence: Medium* (absence-of-evidence based, not independently audited).

**OPPORTUNITY BRIEF 2 — Ride the AI-adoption curve while it's still rising.** Brazil-wide AI shopping usage jumped from ~60% to 77% in a year (Bain), and AI-openness for "casa e decoração" specifically sits at 63% — high but not yet saturated, and most existing AI decor tools solve visualization, not decision ([Bain](https://www.bain.com/pt-br/about/media-center/press-releases/south-america/2026/77-dos-brasileiros-afirmam-utilizar-ia-aponta-relatorio-consumer-pulse-da-bain/)). *Confidence: Medium* — the window narrows the longer Magalu's Lu/WhatsApp da Lu keeps scaling and normalizing AI shopping under a different (retailer-owned, not neutral) model.

**OPPORTUNITY BRIEF 3 — Trust positioning during a visible sector trust crisis.** Procon complaints against furniture retailers are rising sharply and publicly (Marabraz, Tok&Stok, Sofá na Caixa), and 22% of regretful buyers don't even know their legal return rights. A credible, evidence-first "we help you avoid regret" narrative lands at a moment when the category's biggest brands are visibly failing customers in the press. *Confidence: Medium-High* on the sector trust crisis being real and current (strong Procon/Reclame Aqui sourcing); *Low-Medium* on how much of that reputational damage a decision-assistance narrative alone can capture, since (per Section 2) the crisis is driven mostly by logistics, not decision quality.

## 8. What are the three greatest risks?

**RISK ASSESSMENT 1 — Overclaiming what "decision assistance" fixes.** The sector's dominant, most visible, most-complained-about problem is delivery/fulfillment failure, not decision quality (Section 2). If Zupp1's public narrative implies "we solve the trust problem in this category," and customers still experience delivery/assembly failures, the mismatch between promise and outcome is a reputational risk — the exact kind of pattern that produced Tok&Stok's and Sofá na Caixa's 2025 Reclame Aqui spikes. **This directly challenges any existing internal assumption that better decisions alone meaningfully reduce complaint/regret volume — the evidence says logistics dominates that number.** *Confidence: Medium-High.*

**RISK ASSESSMENT 2 — Resource asymmetry with Magazine Luiza.** Magalu already operates a scaled, company-wide, Big-Tech-backed AI conversational commerce system generating millions of interactions and documented 3x conversion lift. If "AI-assisted decision help" becomes the industry's next competitive front (plausible given the AI-adoption trend in Q6/Q7), a company with Magalu's capital and partnerships can extend into "decision assistance" faster than most any independent player. *Confidence: High* that Magalu is currently ahead in AI capability; *Medium* on how quickly that capability would be repositioned toward Zupp1's specific "neutral decision layer" niche versus staying focused on selling from its own catalog.

**RISK ASSESSMENT 3 (also an Intelligence Gap) — The core thesis is unquantified.** Every finding in Section 6 is directionally supportive, but none of it is a direct, causal, first-party measurement of "decision assistance improves outcomes" in this exact market. Continuing to invest heavily on the strength of converging-but-indirect evidence, without closing this gap using Zupp1's own data (return rates, satisfaction, conversion on content-assisted vs. non-assisted purchases), risks building conviction faster than evidence. *Confidence: Medium-High that this gap exists and matters; this is a call to verify, not a verdict against the strategy.*

**A note on Standing Order 5 (wasting time):** the content-gap/SEO-volume question (Section 4) could not be answered with real numbers in this pass — Google Trends and SEMrush required live, authenticated sessions this research could not access. If the next phase (SEO refinement, per the project roadmap) proceeds on assumed keyword volumes rather than a live tool pull, that is the one place in this mission where I'd flag a real risk of the team investing content effort against an unverified target.

## The single most important insight for the bridge this week

**The market problem is real and well evidenced; the "decision-assistance platform" solution is a well-supported hypothesis, not a proven one — and the sector's most visible, most-measured pain point right now is delivery/fulfillment failure, not decision quality.** Every large-sample survey in this research agrees Brazilians research extensively, regret often, and are rapidly turning to AI for exactly this category. But no data source — Brazilian or international — directly measures whether *decision assistance itself* (as opposed to logistics, price, or trust marketing) is what actually moves the needle. Before this becomes the company's full identity, Zupp1 should plan to generate its own first-party evidence (conversion and return-rate differences between assisted and unassisted purchases) rather than relying solely on this externally-sourced, directionally-favorable, but not yet causal picture.
