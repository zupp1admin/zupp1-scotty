# MQ-META-02: Conversion Psychology Intelligence

**Mission:** Focused intelligence on ethical, evidence-supported psychological mechanisms for Zupp1's first Meta campaign, homepage-to-product journey, Biblioteca/educational content, Loja experience, product presentation, trust system, Atendimento, checkout, and measurement plan.
**Governing principle (Commander Spock, MQ-META-02 authorization):** the objective is not to persuade people to buy; it is to help them become sufficiently informed to decide with confidence.
**Central question:** How does trust become action without compromising Zupp1's philosophy ("Toda boa escolha começa com compreensão")?
**Labels used:** FACT (grounded in a fetched source), ASSESSMENT (analytical inference by this mission), RECOMMENDATION (proposed action), INTELLIGENCE GAP (unverified — not to be treated as data).
**Date of research:** 11 August 2026. All numeric claims are cited to pages fetched during this mission.

---

## Executive Summary

1. **FACT — Trust and perceived risk are the operative variables in e-commerce decisions, not persuasion intensity.** A meta-analysis of e-commerce studies finds trust, perceived risk, perceived security and e-WOM all significantly influence purchase decisions, and that perceived risk substantially *moderates* the trust→purchase relationship; the same analysis found no significant effect-size difference between respondents in high-income and low-income countries, suggesting the trust mechanism is not culturally exotic ([Handoyo, *Heliyon* 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11044045/)). **ASSESSMENT (high confidence):** Zupp1's hypothesis "education creates trust" is directionally consistent with the literature, but the literature's causal chain is *uncertainty reduction → trust → action*, not *information volume → trust*. Education only converts when it resolves the specific uncertainty blocking the decision.

2. **FACT — The largest documented conversion losses are informational and structural, not motivational.** Excluding "just browsing," the top reasons US shoppers abandon carts are extra costs too high (40%), slow delivery (20%), distrust of the site with card data (19%), forced account creation (18%), and too long/complicated checkout (17%); 12% abandoned because they could not see or calculate total order cost up front ([Baymard cart abandonment statistics](https://baymard.com/lists/cart-abandonment-rate)). **ASSESSMENT (high confidence):** For a pre-revenue store, total-cost transparency, delivery clarity and guest checkout are higher-yield than any persuasion mechanism.

3. **FACT — In Brazil the payment moment is the single largest abandonment point.** In Visa's Panorama e-commerce study (1,521 online interviews, Brazil, 18–26 Dec 2025, 95% confidence, ±2.5 p.p.), 90% had bought online in the last 30 days, 79% of last purchases were on a smartphone, credit card (47%) and Pix (45%) were nearly tied, and **37% abandon right after choosing the payment method**; 87% said the experience would be better if it completed in ~15 seconds without breaking the flow ([Visa Panorama e-commerce 2026](https://www.visa.com.br/content/dam/VCOM/regional/lac/brazil/visa-conecta/panorama-ecommerce-2026.pdf)).

4. **FACT — Choice overload is real but conditional.** A meta-analysis of 99 observations (N = 7,202) finds the effect of assortment size on choice overload is significant *once moderators are modeled*, and is amplified by high decision-task difficulty, high choice-set complexity, **high preference uncertainty**, and an effort-minimizing goal ([Chernev, Böckenholt & Goodman, *JCP* 2015](https://chernev.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/02/ChoiceOverload_JCP_2015.pdf)). **ASSESSMENT (high confidence):** Zupp1's target visitor — an uncertain buyer of hard-to-evaluate home/decor goods — sits in exactly the moderator profile where overload is most likely. Structuring the choice is the core product, not a nicety.

5. **FACT — Education can also *increase* regret and returns when it becomes overload.** A survey of 536 Chinese online shoppers found perceived information overload positively predicted impulsive buying and cognitive dissonance, which in turn predicted return intention ([Lv & Liu, *IJERPH* 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9265496/)). **ASSESSMENT (moderate confidence, correlational and non-Brazilian):** depth must be *stage-matched* and progressively disclosed, not front-loaded.

6. **FACT — Scarcity cues can damage trust and experience.** In an online experiment (N = 202), limited-time and limited-quantity scarcity cues were associated with *lower perceived vendor benevolence*; a site design without scarcity cues produced superior hedonic and pragmatic user experience, and limited-time cues produced frustration-related negative emotions ([Behaviour & Information Technology, 2023](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/0144929X.2023.2242966)). **RECOMMENDATION (high confidence):** Zupp1 should prohibit fabricated countdowns, fake stock counters and other manufactured urgency. This recommendation combines the study's directional evidence with Zupp1's doctrine; it does not claim that every truthful availability notice is harmful.

7. **FACT — Recommendations build trust only when their basis is disclosed and neutral.** In laboratory experiments (US, replicated in Hong Kong), a biased recommendation agent with sponsorship disclosure produced *lower trust and higher distrust* than a neutral agent, and trust in a biased agent rose only when both explanations for organic recommendations *and* sponsorship disclosure were present — while distrust was never significantly reduced ([Kwon et al., *Management Science* 2018](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2017.2906)). **ASSESSMENT (high confidence):** Zupp1's recommendation logic must be explained and its commercial incentives disclosed; a "helpful advisor" that is silently commercially biased is the fastest available way to destroy the whole thesis.

8. **FACT — A ~R$20/day Meta campaign cannot produce conversion-grade optimization signal.** Meta states an ad set usually exits the learning phase "after about 50 results in the week after the ad set's last significant edit" ([Meta Business Help Center, About the learning phase](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/112167992830700)). **ASSESSMENT (high confidence):** At R$20/day, purchase-optimized delivery will remain learning-limited. The first campaign must be framed and measured as an **ecosystem stress test with qualitative and behavioral diagnostics**, not as a conversion experiment. **What clicks cannot tell you: whether the visitor understood, whether they trusted, or whether the decision was informed.**

**Bottom line (ASSESSMENT, high confidence):** Trust becomes action through *uncertainty removal at the exact moment of hesitation* — total cost visible before commitment, delivery and return terms stated in plain language, a structured (not maximal) comparison, an explained recommendation, a frictionless payment step, and a legally-compliant, honest post-purchase promise. Every mechanism Zupp1 needs is compatible with its editorial doctrine; the mechanisms it must refuse (manufactured scarcity, hidden fees, fabricated proof, forced accounts) are also the ones with the weakest and most reputationally hazardous evidence.

## Evidence Review

Each subsection separates **Evidence** (what sources state) from **Assessment** (this mission's inference).

### 1. Uncertainty reduction, perceived risk, trust and guarantees

**Evidence.**
- Trust, perceived risk, perceived security and e-WOM significantly influence e-commerce purchasing decisions; perceived risk plays a substantial moderating role in the trust→purchase relationship, while perceived security and e-WOM do not moderate it; no significant effect-size difference was found between high- and low-income-country respondents ([Handoyo, *Heliyon* 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11044045/)). *Type: meta-analysis of correlational survey studies.*
- A meta-analysis of 21 papers on return-policy leniency across five dimensions (time, money, effort, scope, exchange) found that **overall, leniency increases purchase more than it increases returns**; specifically, *money* and *effort* leniency increase purchase, *scope* leniency increases returns, and *time* and *exchange* leniency reduce returns ([Janakiraman, Syrdal & Freling, *Journal of Retailing* 2016, abstract](https://econpapers.repec.org/article/eeejouret/v_3a92_3ay_3a2016_3ai_3a2_3ap_3a226-235.htm)).
- Practitioner reporting on the same research team notes that monetary leniency (e.g., a 100% money-back guarantee) aids the purchase decision, and that time/effort leniency may not lift purchases as much but tends to result in fewer returns; a satisfactory return experience lowered perceived risk on future purchases in a field experiment ([American Marketing Association summary of Janakiraman and of Petersen & Kumar 2015](https://www.ama.org/2017/09/20/lenient-return-policies-can-increase-sales/)).
- 19% of US shoppers (excluding "just browsing") abandoned because they did not trust the site with credit-card information, and 13% because the returns policy was unsatisfactory ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/lists/cart-abandonment-rate)).
- In a Thai study of high-involvement online furniture commerce (e-Delphi with 19 experts plus CB-SEM on N = 1,351 valid surveys), the model explicitly separates transactional appraisals (perceived value, satisfaction) from **relational appraisals (customer trust, loyalty)** as distinct organism-layer drivers of continuance purchase intention ([*JTAER* 2026, 21(8), 255](https://www.mdpi.com/0718-1876/21/8/255)). *Type: cross-sectional survey + SEM, Thailand, non-probability sampling.*

**Assessment (high confidence).** Guarantees are best understood as *risk transfer instruments*, not promotions. The strongest evidence supports **monetary and effort leniency** (full refund, low-hassle process) as the purchase-side levers, and **time/exchange leniency** as the returns-control lever. FACT-based caution: the return-leniency meta-analysis is dominated by non-Brazilian retail contexts and mixes lab and field designs; effect magnitudes were not recoverable from the abstract.

### 2. Cognitive load, choice architecture, decision aids and choice overload

**Evidence.**
- Choice overload is moderated by four factors — decision task difficulty, choice-set complexity, **preference uncertainty**, and an effort-minimizing decision goal — each with a reliable and significant impact; with moderators modeled, the overall effect of assortment size is significant, contrary to earlier meta-analytic findings. Satisfaction/confidence, regret, choice deferral and switching likelihood behave as interchangeable measures of overload ([Chernev et al., *JCP* 2015, 99 observations, N = 7,202](https://chernev.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/02/ChoiceOverload_JCP_2015.pdf)).
- The methodological dispute is explicit in the literature: an earlier meta-analysis reported a mean choice-overload effect "virtually zero" with preconditions but no sufficient conditions, prompting the argument that the interesting question is *when* overload occurs rather than *whether* ([Chernev, Böckenholt & Goodman commentary, *JCR* 2010](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/~/media/Files/Faculty/Research/ChoiceOverload_JCR_2010.ashx)).
- Perceived information overload was positively associated with impulsive buying and cognitive dissonance, which predicted online return intention (survey, N = 536 valid of 718 collected, mainland China, Oct–Dec 2021, 7-point Likert, 5,000 bootstrap resamples) ([Lv & Liu, *IJERPH* 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9265496/)).

**Assessment (high confidence).** For Zupp1's category, *preference uncertainty* is the visitor's default state — which is precisely the moderator that makes larger assortments harmful. **RECOMMENDATION:** the Loja should default to a small, reasoned, criteria-labelled set with an explicit "why these" rationale, and expose the full catalogue only on request. Choice deferral and low decision confidence — not bounce rate — are the correct diagnostics of overload.

### 3. Information sequencing and progressive disclosure

**Evidence.**
- Progressive disclosure means initially showing only a few of the most important options and revealing specialized detail on request; it improves learnability, efficiency of use and error rate. Two design conditions must be met: the right split between initial and secondary content, and an obvious mechanism for progressing between levels. An e-commerce example given is showing a few key attributes on the product page with full specifications one click away ([Nielsen Norman Group](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/)). *Type: authoritative UX practitioner guidance, not a controlled experiment.*
- On product pages specifically, Baymard reports 2+ years of testing dedicated only to PDP UX, 1,300+ observed PDP usability issues, 110+ resulting guidelines, a benchmark of 344 leading US/European sites with 18,000+ scored elements, and that only 49% of sites reach "decent" or "good" PDP UX while 51% are "mediocre" or worse; nearly all users pass through a product page before purchasing and it is "often on the product page where users make up their mind" ([Baymard Product Page UX research](https://baymard.com/research/product-page)).
- Information overload is associated with dissonance and return intention (see §2, [Lv & Liu 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9265496/)).

**Assessment (moderate-to-high confidence).** The evidence supports *depth on demand*, not *depth by default*. Education helps when it maps to the uncertainty active at that stage (ad: what problem this solves; homepage: how the method works; Biblioteca: how to judge a category; PDP: how this specific item fits my space, budget and timeline). **INTELLIGENCE GAP:** no fetched source quantifies the conversion effect of educational content depth at each funnel stage; the "how much education is too much" threshold must be discovered by Zupp1's own instrumentation.

### 4. Social proof, authority, and when they backfire or become deceptive

**Evidence.**
- Meta-analysis of 1,532 effect sizes from 96 studies across 40 platforms and 26 product categories: eWOM is positively correlated with sales at **r ≈ .091**; **eWOM volume has a stronger impact on sales than valence**; negative eWOM does not always damage sales, but high variability does; effects are stronger for tangible goods new to the market ([Babić Rosario et al., *JMR* 2016](https://digitalcommons.du.edu/marketing_fac/2/)).
- Analysis of PowerReviews customer data across more than 40 categories: purchase likelihood **peaks when average star rating is between 4.2 and 4.5** and declines as it approaches a perfect 5; negative reviews help establish trust and authenticity; 82% of shoppers specifically seek out negative reviews; star ratings had a stronger influence on expensive items within the same category and are most impactful in high-consideration categories "because reviews mitigate risk"; up to 80% of reviews originate from post-purchase emails ([Medill Spiegel Research Center](https://spiegel.medill.northwestern.edu/star-ratings-and-review-content/)). *Type: observational analysis of retailer data; no sample size disclosed on the page.*
- The US FTC's Rule on the Use of Consumer Reviews and Testimonials took effect 21 October 2024, addresses deceptive and unfair conduct involving reviews and testimonials, and authorizes civil penalties for knowing violations; agencies, PR firms, review brokers and reputation-management companies are not immune; the rule also covers review suppression and fake indicators of social-media influence ([FTC Q&A](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers)). *Applies to the US, not Brazil, but is the clearest published statement of where the ethical/legal line sits.*

**Assessment (high confidence).** The average eWOM–sales correlation (.091) is real but modest; social proof is a *risk-reduction signal*, not a growth engine. For a pre-revenue brand with zero reviews, the honest substitutes are **verifiable authority** (transparent methodology, named expertise, sourced content) and **transparent absence** ("this product has no reviews yet"). Suppressing or manufacturing early reviews would both violate the doctrine and, in the observed 4.2–4.5 pattern, plausibly *reduce* credibility.
**RECOMMENDATION:** publish negative and mixed reviews unfiltered as soon as volume exists; never gate, filter, or incentivize sentiment.

### 5. Price perception, total-cost transparency, scarcity and urgency

**Evidence.**
- 40% of US shoppers (excluding browsers) abandoned because extra costs — shipping, tax, fees — were too high, and 12% because they could not see or calculate the total order cost up front ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/lists/cart-abandonment-rate)).
- The FTC's final Unfair or Deceptive Fees rulemaking record documents the harm of hidden mandatory fees: commenters described fee omission and misrepresentation as practices that "erode trust and hinder informed decision-making"; Attorneys General from 19 states and DC described bait-and-switch pricing and misleading fees as "a chronic, prolific problem"; a GAO non-generalizable sample found live-event ticketing fees averaging 27% of ticket price in primary markets and 31% in secondary markets; the NPRM drew 60,853 comments ([FTC final rule document](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/r207011_udf_rule_2024_final_0.pdf)).
- Scarcity cues (limited-time and limited-quantity) were associated with perceived **lack of vendor benevolence**; designs without scarcity cues delivered superior hedonic and pragmatic UX; limited-time cues elicited frustration-related negative emotions. The paper situates scarcity cues within the "dark patterns" literature and notes consumers often cannot verify whether scarcity is genuine (online experiment, N = 202) ([*Behaviour & Information Technology*, 2023](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/0144929X.2023.2242966)).
- Brazilian law is explicit on price composition: e-commerce sites must display, prominently and clearly, the **itemized discrimination in the price of any additional or accessory costs such as delivery or insurance**, plus the full conditions of the offer including payment modalities, availability, and delivery deadline, and clear and conspicuous information on any restrictions to the offer ([Decreto nº 7.962/2013, Art. 2º](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2011-2014/2013/decreto/d7962.htm)).
- Free shipping is a top decision factor in Brazil: 62% cited free shipping as among the most influential factors in the purchase decision, ahead of product quality (56%) and competitive pricing (53%) ([CX Trends 2025, Octadesk/Opinion Box, as reported by E-Commerce Update](https://www.ecommerceupdate.org/en/news/research/ranking-mostra-principais-queixas-dos-consumidores-brasileiros/)). *Type: market survey via secondary report.*

**Assessment (high confidence).** Total-cost transparency is simultaneously (a) the largest documented abandonment lever, (b) a Brazilian legal obligation, and (c) fully aligned with Zupp1's doctrine. Scarcity/urgency is the inverse on all three counts.
**RECOMMENDATION:** show delivered total (product + frete + any fee) as early as the product page for the visitor's CEP; never introduce a cost after the cart. **Prohibit** countdowns, "X pessoas vendo agora", and recurring "last chance" framing.

### 6. Commitment, micro-conversions, goal gradient, checkout friction and loss aversion

**Evidence.**
- Goal-gradient effects are documented in real purchase behavior: in a café reward program with 949 completed cards and nearly ten thousand coffee purchases, average inter-purchase time fell by **20% (0.7 days, t = 2.6, p < .05)** from first to last purchase; observed card completion took 24.6 days versus 29.4 days at the initial rate (~16% faster). Customers given a 12-stamp card with 2 pre-existing bonus stamps completed the 10 required purchases faster than those with a plain 10-stamp card ("illusionary goal progress"), and stronger acceleration predicted a higher probability of earning a second reward and faster reengagement. Alternative explanations (habituation, expiration concerns, time trends, heterogeneity bias) were tested and rejected ([Kivetz, Urminsky & Zheng, *JMR* 2006](https://www.columbia.edu/~rk566/Session4/Goal-Gradient_Illusionary_Goal_Progress.pdf)).
- Checkout friction is quantified: Baymard's running average documented cart abandonment rate is **70.22%** across 50 studies (last updated 22 September 2025); 42% of US online shoppers have abandoned because they were "just browsing / not ready to buy" ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/lists/cart-abandonment-rate)).
- In Baymard's 2025 checkout benchmark, 64% of desktop sites and 63% of mobile sites have "mediocre" or worse checkout UX, only 2% rate "good," and none rate "perfect"; the average large ecommerce site can gain **as much as a 35% increase in conversion rate** from checkout design changes alone. 18% of 1,026 surveyed US online shoppers abandoned an order because they did not want to create an account, and **62% of sites fail to make guest checkout the most prominent option**; an ideal checkout can be as short as 12–14 form elements while the average US checkout shows 23.48 by default ([Baymard, Checkout UX](https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-of-checkout-ux); [Baymard cart abandonment](https://baymard.com/lists/cart-abandonment-rate)).

**Assessment (high confidence).** Goal-gradient evidence is strong but was generated in *reward-program* contexts with repeated purchases; extrapolating it to a first-time single-purchase checkout is an inference, not a finding. The ethical version for Zupp1 is **honest progress feedback** (clear step indicators, saved progress, no surprise steps) — not artificial progress inflation or loss-framed nudges.
**RECOMMENDATION:** guest checkout prominent by default; minimize form elements; never introduce new information (cost, delivery date, restriction) after the visitor commits.
**INTELLIGENCE GAP:** no fetched source quantifies loss-aversion framing effects in a real e-commerce checkout; treat loss framing as unvalidated and, given doctrine, out of scope.

### 7. Message/landing-page congruence and expectation confirmation

**Evidence.**
- A documented field A/B test (Dallas Theological Seminary, Facebook ads, 1 Jul–29 Aug 2016) increasing ad-to-landing-page image congruence lifted conversion from 0.49% to 0.73%, a **47.6% relative increase**, with total sample 239,607 against a required 9,488 and confidence reported at 100% ([NextAfter experiment #4854](https://www.nextafter.com/experiments/how-congruence-between-ad-and-landing-page-affects-conversion/)). *Type: single-organization practitioner A/B test in a nonprofit education context; external validity to Brazilian home/decor commerce is unestablished.*
- Brazilian law requires that e-commerce contracting respect **fulfilment of the conditions of the offer**, with delivery of the products and services contracted, observing deadlines, quantity, quality and adequacy ([Decreto nº 7.962/2013, Art. 6º](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2011-2014/2013/decreto/d7962.htm)), and that product offers carry correct, clear, precise and conspicuous information in Portuguese about characteristics, quality, quantity, composition, price, guarantee, validity and origin ([CDC, Lei 8.078/1990, Art. 31](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/l8078compilado.htm)).
- Misleading advertising was cited by 24% of Brazilian consumers as a problem experienced ([CX Trends 2025 via E-Commerce Update](https://www.ecommerceupdate.org/en/news/research/ranking-mostra-principais-queixas-dos-consumidores-brasileiros/)).

**Assessment (moderate confidence).** Congruence is best treated by Zupp1 as an *expectation-integrity* requirement rather than a conversion trick: the ad states a promise ("understand before you choose"), and the landing experience must deliver that exact promise as its first visible element. Weak evidentiary base (one practitioner test) but strong doctrinal and legal alignment.
**INTELLIGENCE GAP:** no peer-reviewed, Brazil- or category-relevant quantification of ad→page congruence effects was verified in this mission.

### 8. Home / furniture / decor and high-consideration purchases

**Evidence.**
- Furniture is treated in the literature as a **high-involvement product that consumers prefer to inspect physically**, which is why continuance purchase intention is hard to sustain online; the study models trust and loyalty as a distinct relational layer alongside perceived value and satisfaction (mixed methods: 19-expert three-round e-Delphi with 100% retention; CB-SEM on N = 1,351 valid responses from 1,500 initiated, Thailand, Aug–Dec 2025, purposive non-probability sampling recruited via Facebook/LINE) ([*JTAER* 2026, 21(8), 255](https://www.mdpi.com/0718-1876/21/8/255)).
- Star ratings exert a **stronger influence on expensive items** and are "most impactful for expensive products within high consideration categories, because reviews mitigate risk"; when a consumer has less information about a category, product or brand, more information in the form of reviews increases purchase likelihood ([Medill Spiegel](https://spiegel.medill.northwestern.edu/star-ratings-and-review-content/)).
- Product-page design carries disproportionate weight in exactly this kind of decision: nearly all users pass through a PDP and it is often where they decide; 51% of 344 benchmarked leading sites have "mediocre" or worse PDP UX ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/research/product-page)).

**Assessment (moderate confidence).** The category evidence converges on the same mechanism as the general evidence: the binding constraint is *evaluability* — can the buyer judge fit, scale, material and durability without touching it. Zupp1's differentiation should be evaluability infrastructure (dimensions in context, material explanation, care and durability, "who this is not for"), not persuasion.
**INTELLIGENCE GAP:** claims circulating about AR/3D visualization lifting furniture conversion and cutting returns were found only on vendor/agency pages during this mission and were **not** verified against a primary study; do not use any AR/3D percentage figure until a primary source is fetched.

### 9. Brazil-specific evidence

**Evidence — market and behavior.**
- Brazil's e-commerce sector was expected to reach US$36.3B in revenue in 2025, with 94M Brazilians expected to purchase online in 2025 (up 3M vs. 2024, per ABCOMM); the top platforms by market share cited are Mercado Livre (35%), Amazon Brasil (20%), Shopee (15%), Magazine Luiza (10%), Americanas/B2W (8%); the top social platforms are WhatsApp (169M users), YouTube (142M), Instagram (113M), Facebook (109M) and TikTok (82M); Pix is described as the most used payment method in the country, expected to surpass credit cards as the most widely used online payment method in 2025, while credit cards remain accepted by 98.3% of online retailers ([US ITA, Brazil Country Commercial Guide — eCommerce](https://www.trade.gov/country-commercial-guides/brazil-ecommerce)). *Type: government commercial guide summarizing third-party market data.*
- Visa's Brazilian consumer study (N = 1,521, online, 18–26 Dec 2025, ±2.5 p.p. at 95%): 90% bought online in the last 30 days and 34% buy weekly; smartphones (Android + iPhone) accounted for 79% of last purchases; credit card 47% vs. Pix 45% on the last purchase; **37% abandon immediately after choosing the payment method**; 87% said the experience would be better if it concluded in about 15 seconds without breaking the flow; among the 51% of Brazilians who had already faced fraud in e-commerce, Pix was the method used in the incident in 62% of cases ([Visa Panorama e-commerce 2026](https://www.visa.com.br/content/dam/VCOM/regional/lac/brazil/visa-conecta/panorama-ecommerce-2026.pdf)). *Type: industry consumer survey by a payments incumbent — note the commercial interest in checkout-friction conclusions.*
- Pix is a Banco Central-operated public digital payment infrastructure; the BCB's second Pix management report covers evolution from launch in November 2020 through December 2025, with security treated as a pillar and a permanent security-rules improvement agenda coordinated through the GE-Seg group ([BCB, Relatório de Gestão do Pix 2023–2025](https://www.bcb.gov.br/content/estabilidadefinanceira/pix/relatorio_de_gestao_pix/relatorio_gestao_pix_2026.pdf)).
- Consumer pain points (CX Trends 2025, Octadesk + Opinion Box): lower-than-expected product/service quality (26%), late deliveries (24%), non-delivery (21%), misleading advertising (24%), customer-service problems (20%), no feedback on complaints (18%). Consumers want fast problem resolution (37%), more shipping options (37%), discount coupons (33%) and shorter delivery times (32%). 77% bought both online and in physical stores in the last 12 months; main channels are online stores (68%), marketplaces (66%), physical stores (64%), with WhatsApp (30%) and Instagram (28%) prominent in decision-making ([E-Commerce Update report](https://www.ecommerceupdate.org/en/news/research/ranking-mostra-principais-queixas-dos-consumidores-brasileiros/)). *Type: commercial market survey via secondary reporting; underlying methodology not verified in this mission.*

**Evidence — legal and institutional trust infrastructure.**
- **Right of regret:** the consumer may withdraw from the contract **within 7 days** from signature or from receipt of the product/service whenever contracting occurs outside the commercial establishment, and amounts paid during the reflection period must be returned immediately and monetarily updated ([CDC, Lei 8.078/1990, Art. 49](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/l8078compilado.htm)).
- **Information duty:** offers must ensure correct, clear, precise, conspicuous information in Portuguese on characteristics, quality, quantity, composition, price, guarantee, validity, origin and health/safety risks ([CDC Art. 31](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/l8078compilado.htm)).
- **E-commerce specifics:** sites must display prominently the supplier's business name and CNPJ/CPF, physical and electronic address, essential product characteristics, itemized additional costs (delivery, insurance), full offer conditions including payment modalities, availability and delivery deadline, and any restrictions; must present a contract summary before contracting with limiting clauses emphasized, provide error-correction tools before finalization, immediately confirm receipt of acceptance, maintain an effective electronic customer-service channel able to handle information, doubts, complaints, suspension or cancellation — with supplier responses to such demands sent **within five days** — and use effective security mechanisms for payment and data. The means to exercise the right of regret must be stated clearly and conspicuously, exercisable through the same tool used for contracting, with accessory contracts rescinded at no cost and immediate confirmation of receipt of the withdrawal ([Decreto nº 7.962/2013, Arts. 2º, 4º, 5º](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2011-2014/2013/decreto/d7962.htm)). Non-compliance triggers the sanctions of CDC Art. 56 (Art. 7º).
- **Public reputation infrastructure:** Consumidor.gov.br, managed by Senacon, finalized 2,328,863 complaints in 2025, with approximately 81.43% resolved positively by companies and an average response time of 6 days ([Boletim Consumidor.gov.br 2025, Ministério da Justiça](https://www.gov.br/mj/pt-br/assuntos/seus-direitos/consumidor/defesadoconsumidor/Biblioteca/boletins-1/boletim-consumidor-gov-br-2025-vfinal.pdf)). Procon-SP publishes an annual Cadastro de Reclamações Fundamentadas under CDC Art. 44, covering complaints finalized by Fundação Procon SP and 126 participating municipal Procons in 2025, ranked by total complaints and unresolved complaints ([Procon-SP](https://www.procon.sp.gov.br/reclamacoes-fundamentadas-2025/)).

**Assessment (high confidence).** In Brazil, "trust signals" are not decorative: much of what conversion literature calls trust-building is **statutory disclosure**. Zupp1 can convert compliance into differentiation by presenting these obligations as plain-language commitments rather than buried legal text. **ASSESSMENT (moderate confidence):** because complaint and resolution data are publicly searchable on Consumidor.gov.br and Procon-SP, post-purchase behavior in Brazil is reputationally visible in a way that makes short-term conversion tricks unusually costly.

### 10. Mobile implications

**Evidence.**
- Baymard's 2026 mobile benchmark of 150+ leading US/European ecommerce sites (71,000+ manually reviewed mobile elements, 56,000+ categorized examples) rates the average mobile site UX "mediocre": 75% of sites are "mediocre," 95% are "mediocre" or "decent," exactly 1 site is "Good" and none are "Perfect." Mobile generates around three-quarters of retail store visits. Baymard notes the benchmarked sites are the biggest sites with large UX budgets, so the problem is "likely even worse for smaller mobile ecommerce sites." Specific gaps include 93% of sites not using adaptive validation error messages, 94% not highlighting the current scope in main navigation, 66% not displaying applied filters in an overview, 57% not letting users navigate reviews via reviewer-submitted images, and 54% lacking an address validator/lookup ([Baymard, Mobile UX Trends 2026](https://baymard.com/blog/mobile-ux-ecommerce)).
- In Brazil, 79% of last online purchases were made on a smartphone, and the payment step is where 37% abandon ([Visa Panorama e-commerce 2026](https://www.visa.com.br/content/dam/VCOM/regional/lac/brazil/visa-conecta/panorama-ecommerce-2026.pdf)).

**Assessment (high confidence).** Zupp1's first campaign will be, in practice, a **mobile campaign**. Educational depth that reads well on desktop can become a load problem on a phone; comparison structures, filters, and error handling are the mobile-specific expressions of "compreensão." Address entry (CEP) and payment are the two highest-risk mobile moments.

### 11. Experimental implications for a low-budget, low-volume first campaign

**Evidence.**
- Meta: ad sets exit the learning phase "as soon as they can deliver stably," usually "after about 50 results in the week after the ad set's last significant edit"; during learning, ad sets are less stable with usually higher CPA and "your results aren't necessarily indicative of future performance"; editing during learning resets it; high ad volume means the system learns less about each ad set; unrealistically small budgets give the delivery system "an inaccurate indicator" of whom to optimize for; ad sets not getting enough results show "Learning limited" ([Meta Business Help Center](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/112167992830700)).
- Baseline outcome rates make small-sample inference weak: documented average cart abandonment is 70.22% ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/lists/cart-abandonment-rate)), and 42% of shoppers abandon simply because they were "just browsing / not ready to buy" ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/lists/cart-abandonment-rate)).

**Assessment (high confidence).** At ~R$20/day, purchase-optimized delivery will almost certainly sit in "Learning limited," and any purchase-rate comparison between creatives will be statistically uninterpretable. **What cannot be inferred from clicks:** comprehension, trust, decision confidence, informedness of the purchase, or the causal effect of educational content. **RECOMMENDATION:** design the first campaign around *observable behavioral evidence of understanding* (content depth reached, comparison tool used, CEP/frete checked, guide-to-product transitions, Atendimento questions asked) plus *qualitative* evidence (the actual text of WhatsApp/Atendimento questions), and treat purchases as a stress-test signal on the ecosystem rather than as the metric under optimization.

## Best Practices (evidence-supported, doctrine-compatible)

1. **Publish the delivered total early.** Product + frete for the visitor's CEP + any fee, before the cart. Grounded in the 40%/12% abandonment findings ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/lists/cart-abandonment-rate)) and required by [Decreto 7.962/2013 Art. 2º IV](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2011-2014/2013/decreto/d7962.htm).
2. **State delivery in dates, not ranges of hope.** Slow delivery is the #2 non-browsing abandonment reason ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/lists/cart-abandonment-rate)); late/non-delivery are top Brazilian complaints (24%/21%) ([CX Trends 2025 via E-Commerce Update](https://www.ecommerceupdate.org/en/news/research/ranking-mostra-principais-queixas-dos-consumidores-brasileiros/)).
3. **Make the guarantee monetary and low-effort, and say so in the same visual block as the price** — money and effort leniency are the purchase-side levers ([Janakiraman et al. 2016](https://econpapers.repec.org/article/eeejouret/v_3a92_3ay_3a2016_3ai_3a2_3ap_3a226-235.htm)); the 7-day right of regret is a legal floor, not a marketing claim ([CDC Art. 49](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/l8078compilado.htm)).
4. **Curate, then expose.** Small reasoned sets by default, with the criteria named and full catalogue available — because preference uncertainty is the strongest overload moderator ([Chernev et al. 2015](https://chernev.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/02/ChoiceOverload_JCP_2015.pdf)).
5. **Layer information: summary → detail on request**, with an obvious progression mechanism ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/)).
6. **Explain every recommendation and disclose commercial interest** — explanations plus disclosure were jointly necessary to raise trust in a non-neutral agent, and distrust could not be repaired ([Kwon et al. 2018](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2017.2906)).
7. **Guest checkout prominent; minimum fields; no surprises after commitment** — 18% abandon over forced accounts, 62% of sites bury guest checkout, ideal flows are 12–14 elements vs. 23.48 average ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-of-checkout-ux), [Baymard](https://baymard.com/lists/cart-abandonment-rate)).
8. **Treat the payment step as a first-class design surface** with Pix and card, fast and unbroken — 37% of Brazilians abandon right after choosing the method, 87% want ~15-second completion ([Visa 2026](https://www.visa.com.br/content/dam/VCOM/regional/lac/brazil/visa-conecta/panorama-ecommerce-2026.pdf)).
9. **Design mobile-first for comprehension**, including adaptive validation errors, visible applied filters, and address lookup — the most-failed guidelines in the 2026 mobile benchmark ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/mobile-ux-ecommerce)).
10. **When reviews arrive, publish the negative ones.** Purchase likelihood peaks at 4.2–4.5 stars and 82% of shoppers seek negative reviews ([Spiegel](https://spiegel.medill.northwestern.edu/star-ratings-and-review-content/)); suppression and fabrication are precisely the conduct targeted by the FTC's rule ([FTC Q&A](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers)).
11. **Make Atendimento a documented, responsive channel** — Brazilian e-commerce rules require an effective electronic service channel with responses within five days ([Decreto 7.962/2013 Art. 4º](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2011-2014/2013/decreto/d7962.htm)); the national benchmark for resolution behavior is 81.43% positive resolution at a 6-day average response ([Boletim Consumidor.gov.br 2025](https://www.gov.br/mj/pt-br/assuntos/seus-direitos/consumidor/defesadoconsumidor/Biblioteca/boletins-1/boletim-consumidor-gov-br-2025-vfinal.pdf)).

## Applicability to Zupp1 (Bridge Implications)

**How trust becomes action, in Zupp1's terms.** The evidence supports a four-step chain, each step observable:

1. **Congruent promise** → the visitor arrives expecting understanding, and the first screen delivers understanding rather than a sales pitch (expectation integrity; [CDC Art. 31](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/l8078compilado.htm), [NextAfter](https://www.nextafter.com/experiments/how-congruence-between-ad-and-landing-page-affects-conversion/)).
2. **Structured evaluability** → the visitor can compare a small, reasoned set on criteria they now understand, reducing preference uncertainty, the strongest overload moderator ([Chernev et al. 2015](https://chernev.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/02/ChoiceOverload_JCP_2015.pdf)).
3. **Risk removal at the decision point** → total delivered cost, delivery date, return terms, and an explained recommendation, all visible before commitment ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/lists/cart-abandonment-rate); [Janakiraman et al. 2016](https://econpapers.repec.org/article/eeejouret/v_3a92_3ay_3a2016_3ai_3a2_3ap_3a226-235.htm); [Kwon et al. 2018](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2017.2906)).
4. **Frictionless, honest completion** → guest checkout, few fields, fast Pix/card payment, statutory confirmations ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-of-checkout-ux); [Visa](https://www.visa.com.br/content/dam/VCOM/regional/lac/brazil/visa-conecta/panorama-ecommerce-2026.pdf); [Decreto 7.962/2013](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2011-2014/2013/decreto/d7962.htm)).

**Short-term conversion tactics vs. sustainable trust (ASSESSMENT, high confidence).** The tactics with the best short-run reputation (urgency, hidden-fee-driven low headline prices, curated-positive reviews, forced accounts) are each contradicted by the strongest available evidence *on their own terms* — worse UX and lower perceived benevolence for scarcity ([BIT 2023](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/0144929X.2023.2242966)), 40% abandonment for surprise costs ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/lists/cart-abandonment-rate)), a credibility ceiling above 4.5 stars ([Spiegel](https://spiegel.medill.northwestern.edu/star-ratings-and-review-content/)), 18% abandonment for forced accounts ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-of-checkout-ux)). Zupp1 does not face a trade-off between ethics and conversion here; it faces a trade-off between *ethics* and *the illusion of conversion*.

**When recommendations increase confidence vs. create resistance (ASSESSMENT, moderate-high confidence).** Confidence rises when the recommendation is explained, its criteria are inspectable, and its commercial interest is disclosed; resistance and distrust rise when the recommendation is directive without a visible basis, and disclosure alone does not repair distrust in a biased agent ([Kwon et al. 2018](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2017.2906)). A psychological-reactance literature specific to online recommendation services exists ([Lee & Lee, *Decision Support Systems*, 2009](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0378720609000962)) but its findings could not be retrieved in this mission — treat the "directive advice triggers reactance" mechanism as plausible-but-unverified here.

### Page/journey implications

**Ad promise (Meta).**
- The ad must promise exactly what the landing experience delivers first — understanding, not a discount ([CDC Art. 31](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/l8078compilado.htm), [Decreto 7.962/2013 Art. 6º](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2011-2014/2013/decreto/d7962.htm)).
- No urgency, no scarcity, no price claim that the page cannot immediately substantiate ([BIT 2023](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/0144929X.2023.2242966)).
- RECOMMENDATION: hold creative and audience stable long enough to avoid resetting learning ([Meta](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/112167992830700)).

**Homepage.**
- Answer three questions above the fold: what Zupp1 does, how it decides what to recommend, and what it costs to receive something in São Paulo.
- Show the method, not the manifesto: one visible path into the Biblioteca and one into the Loja.
- Trust block: CNPJ, physical/electronic address, contact channel — statutorily required prominence ([Decreto 7.962/2013 Art. 2º](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2011-2014/2013/decreto/d7962.htm)).

**Biblioteca / educational guides.**
- Structure each guide around a decision, not a topic: "how to judge X," "what usually goes wrong," "who this is not for."
- Progressive disclosure: summary answer first, evidence and nuance on request ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/)).
- End every guide with a *criteria-based* bridge into a small comparison set, never with a single hard-sell CTA.
- Guard against overload: depth is opt-in, because overload correlates with dissonance and return intention ([Lv & Liu 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9265496/)).

**Loja experience (category/comparison).**
- Default to a small reasoned set with named criteria; expose the full set on request ([Chernev et al. 2015](https://chernev.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/02/ChoiceOverload_JCP_2015.pdf)).
- Show applied filters in an overview and highlight current navigation scope — among the most-failed mobile guidelines ([Baymard 2026](https://baymard.com/blog/mobile-ux-ecommerce)).
- Publish the ranking logic ("por que estes") and disclose any commercial interest ([Kwon et al. 2018](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2017.2906)).

**Product page.**
- Delivered total for the visitor's CEP, delivery date, return terms and guarantee in the same decision block as the price ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/lists/cart-abandonment-rate); [Decreto 7.962/2013 Art. 2º IV–V](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2011-2014/2013/decreto/d7962.htm)).
- Evaluability content: dimensions in context, materials, care, durability, limitations — the PDP is where users decide ([Baymard PDP research](https://baymard.com/research/product-page)).
- Zero-review honesty while pre-revenue; when reviews exist, allow negatives and image-based review browsing ([Spiegel](https://spiegel.medill.northwestern.edu/star-ratings-and-review-content/); [Baymard 2026](https://baymard.com/blog/mobile-ux-ecommerce)).

**Cart / checkout.**
- Guest checkout as the most prominent option; minimal fields; no cost or condition introduced after commitment ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-of-checkout-ux)).
- Pix and card, with the payment step engineered as the highest-risk moment ([Visa](https://www.visa.com.br/content/dam/VCOM/regional/lac/brazil/visa-conecta/panorama-ecommerce-2026.pdf)).
- Honest progress indicators and error-correction tools before finalization; immediate confirmation of acceptance ([Decreto 7.962/2013 Art. 4º](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2011-2014/2013/decreto/d7962.htm)) — progress feedback should be accurate, never inflated ([Kivetz et al. 2006](https://www.columbia.edu/~rk566/Session4/Goal-Gradient_Illusionary_Goal_Progress.pdf)).

**Atendimento.**
- Effective electronic service channel handling information, doubts, complaints, suspension and cancellation, with responses within five days and immediate acknowledgment ([Decreto 7.962/2013 Art. 4º](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2011-2014/2013/decreto/d7962.htm)).
- WhatsApp matters in Brazilian decision-making (30% cite it in the purchase process; 169M WhatsApp users) ([CX Trends 2025 via E-Commerce Update](https://www.ecommerceupdate.org/en/news/research/ranking-mostra-principais-queixas-dos-consumidores-brasileiros/); [US ITA](https://www.trade.gov/country-commercial-guides/brazil-ecommerce)).
- ASSESSMENT: Atendimento transcripts are Zupp1's highest-value early research asset — they reveal the *actual* uncertainty blocking decisions, which clicks cannot.

**Post-purchase.**
- Clear, conspicuous instructions for the 7-day right of regret, exercisable through the same channel used to buy, with immediate acknowledgment ([CDC Art. 49](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/l8078compilado.htm); [Decreto 7.962/2013 Art. 5º](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2011-2014/2013/decreto/d7962.htm)).
- Post-purchase review solicitation is the standard origin of review volume (up to 80% of reviews) ([Spiegel](https://spiegel.medill.northwestern.edu/star-ratings-and-review-content/)) — request reviews without conditioning them on sentiment ([FTC Q&A](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers)).
- A satisfactory return experience is associated with lower perceived risk on future purchases ([AMA summary of Petersen & Kumar 2015](https://www.ama.org/2017/09/20/lenient-return-policies-can-increase-sales/)).

Therefore these are **diagnostic hypotheses with behavioral evidence**, not powered conversion tests.

| # | Hypothesis | Controlled variable | Primary observable behavior | Supporting diagnostics | Falsification / stop signal | Decision improved |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | **Understanding-first congruence**: an ad promising understanding, landing on a page whose first element delivers understanding, produces qualified engagement rather than bounce | Ad promise type (understanding-led vs. product-led), landing element held constant | Scroll-to-criteria depth; entry into a guide or comparison within the first session | Time to first meaningful interaction; return visits within 7 days | Sessions reach the page but do not engage with any explanatory element (>80% single-element sessions) over 2+ weeks | Whether the ad promise is the right entry contract |
| H2 | **Stage-matched education**: guide → criteria → small comparison produces more *considered* product views than direct-to-product entry | Entry path (Biblioteca-first vs. Loja-first) | Guide→product transitions; comparison tool use; product pages viewed per session with dwell above a comprehension threshold | Atendimento questions referencing guide content; CEP/frete checks | Guide readers never transition to products, or transition and immediately exit — education consumed but not usable | Whether "education creates trust" holds behaviorally |
| H3 | **Cost transparency reduces late abandonment**: showing delivered total + delivery date on the PDP shifts abandonment earlier and reduces cart-stage drop | Placement of delivered total (PDP vs. cart) | Cart-stage abandonment rate; CEP entries on PDP | Add-to-cart rate; time between CEP check and add-to-cart | Cart-stage abandonment unchanged *and* PDP exits rise sharply — cost display is deterring without informing | Where in the journey cost belongs |
| H4 | **Explained recommendation increases decision confidence**: recommendations with visible criteria and disclosure outperform unexplained recommendations on engagement quality | Presence of "por que recomendamos isto" panel | Panel opens; subsequent comparison narrowing (fewer, longer product views) | Atendimento questions shifting from "which one" to "confirm this one" | Panel opened frequently but followed by session abandonment — explanations increasing doubt rather than confidence | Whether Zupp1's recommender helps or triggers resistance |
| H5 | **Payment-step integrity**: a fast, unbroken Pix/card step with guest checkout is not the abandonment point | Checkout configuration (guest-prominent, minimal fields) | Drop-off rate at the payment-method step specifically | Field-level error rates; mobile vs. desktop split | Payment-step drop-off approaches the 37% Brazilian benchmark ([Visa](https://www.visa.com.br/content/dam/VCOM/regional/lac/brazil/visa-conecta/panorama-ecommerce-2026.pdf)) despite low volume | Whether checkout or upstream comprehension is the binding constraint |

**What cannot be inferred from this campaign (FACT/ASSESSMENT, high confidence).**
- Whether one creative is "better" — results during learning are explicitly not indicative of future performance ([Meta](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/112167992830700)).
- Any conversion-rate estimate; the base rate is dominated by non-purchase intent (42% "just browsing") ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/lists/cart-abandonment-rate)).
- Whether visitors understood or trusted anything. Clicks measure attention allocation, not comprehension. Comprehension must come from Atendimento transcripts, on-page behavior sequences, and direct qualitative follow-up.

**RECOMMENDATION (measurement plan).** Instrument micro-conversions as *evidence of understanding*, not as conversion proxies: guide depth reached, criteria panel opened, comparison used, CEP checked, delivery date viewed, return policy opened, Atendimento initiated. Pre-register, before spending, which of these would count as support or falsification for H1–H5.

## Strategic Recommendations

1. **RECOMMENDATION (high confidence): make total-cost and delivery transparency the first build priority**, ahead of any persuasion feature — largest documented abandonment lever and a Brazilian legal requirement ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/lists/cart-abandonment-rate); [Decreto 7.962/2013](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2011-2014/2013/decreto/d7962.htm)).
2. **RECOMMENDATION (high confidence): codify a "no dark patterns" clause in the editorial doctrine**, naming countdowns, fake stock counters, manufactured urgency, hidden fees, forced accounts, and filtered reviews as prohibited — each is contradicted by evidence as well as doctrine ([BIT 2023](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/0144929X.2023.2242966); [FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/r207011_udf_rule_2024_final_0.pdf); [FTC Q&A](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers); [Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-of-checkout-ux)).
3. **RECOMMENDATION (high confidence): publish the recommendation methodology** and disclose commercial relationships at the point of recommendation — trust in a non-neutral recommender required both explanation and disclosure, and distrust proved unrepairable ([Kwon et al. 2018](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2017.2906)).
4. **RECOMMENDATION (high confidence): design the guarantee as money-back + low-effort, with bounded time and scope**, and only after the contribution-margin definition is verified — money/effort leniency lift purchases, scope leniency lifts returns ([Janakiraman et al. 2016](https://econpapers.repec.org/article/eeejouret/v_3a92_3ay_3a2016_3ai_3a2_3ap_3a226-235.htm)).
5. **RECOMMENDATION (high confidence): build the first release mobile-first**, with CEP entry, filters, adaptive validation errors and payment treated as the critical path ([Baymard 2026](https://baymard.com/blog/mobile-ux-ecommerce); [Visa](https://www.visa.com.br/content/dam/VCOM/regional/lac/brazil/visa-conecta/panorama-ecommerce-2026.pdf)).
6. **RECOMMENDATION (moderate-high confidence): treat Atendimento as the primary early research instrument**, with question logging and thematic coding, because it is the only channel that reveals residual uncertainty directly.
7. **RECOMMENDATION (moderate confidence): verify eligibility to participate in Consumidor.gov.br and use its national response statistics as directional benchmarks**, not as Zupp1 targets until category and company-size comparability are established. The 2025 platform-wide figures were 81.43% positive resolution and a 6-day average response ([Boletim Consumidor.gov.br 2025](https://www.gov.br/mj/pt-br/assuntos/seus-direitos/consumidor/defesadoconsumidor/Biblioteca/boletins-1/boletim-consumidor-gov-br-2025-vfinal.pdf)).
8. **RECOMMENDATION (high confidence): reframe campaign success criteria before launch** — the first campaign's deliverable is a validated ecosystem and a list of the actual uncertainties blocking São Paulo buyers, not a CPA ([Meta](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/112167992830700)).
9. **RECOMMENDATION (moderate confidence): close Intelligence Gaps 1, 2, 3 and 6 before the second campaign cycle**; Gap 6 (contribution-margin definition) is internal and should be closed immediately, since it constrains guarantee, free-shipping and budget decisions.
